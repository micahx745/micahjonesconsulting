CLAIM UNDER TEST (as given):
  WorldSwitcher writes --cw-bg/--cw-fg/--cw-accent onto the single ancestor
  div wrapping the entire page, and 40+ descendant selectors across
  globals.css reference them, so every write forces a page-wide style
  recalculation instead of a local one. One trace showed UpdateLayoutTree
  as the single largest bucket (532.9ms), recurring every animation frame
  (11-15ms each) for the whole scroll rather than as two isolated spikes
  at the two actual world-switch moments. Letting the browser skip
  layout/paint work for off-screen [data-world] sections should localize
  that cost (symptom: scroll-not-smooth).

METRIC (as given): UpdateLayoutTree total ms in a scripted top-to-Audit
scroll trace (baseline 485-533ms across runs quoted by the task) AND its
per-frame/temporal distribution: currently a near-constant 11-15ms cost
recurring broadly across the scroll; with the fix it should drop in total
AND concentrate almost entirely at the two frames where a real
world-switch write occurs, rather than recurring broadly. This is a
two-part, compound metric -- both parts are evaluated below. Instrument:
scroll-probe.mjs's own --trace output (topTraceEventsByDuration, unmodified
from T3's usage) for the total, plus analyze-trace.mjs (unmodified scratch
tool already in this folder from the S1/T3 lineage) run against each of
the 6 raw --trace JSON files for the 200ms-bucketed temporal distribution.
Same gesture mechanism, cpu=4, no --net (this is the scroll-jank symptom,
not words-slow, so the task's own flag rule does not add --net here).

NEUTRALIZER (exp-T4.css, this folder, exactly as given in the task):
  [data-mode="cw"] section[data-world] {
    content-visibility: auto;
    contain-intrinsic-size: 1200px;
  }

NEUTRALIZER VALIDITY CHECK (verify-T4-inject.mjs, exp-T4-verify-inject.txt):
  Ran once WITHOUT and once WITH exp-T4.css against the live page. The
  selector matches exactly 4 live section[data-world] elements (bone,
  terracotta, espresso, petrol -- matches the task's stated home order).
  Computed content-visibility flips from "visible" (default, no injected
  CSS) to "auto" on all 4, and computed containIntrinsicSize flips from
  "none" to "auto 1200px" on all 4, only when the CSS is injected. The
  neutralizer is confirmed live and non-inert before trusting the A/B
  numbers below.

=== MOVEMENT vs NOISE FLOOR, PREDICTED DIRECTION ===
Predicted: UpdateLayoutTree total drops AND concentrates into two isolated
frames; framesOver33/50ms and p95 read smoother.
Actual:
  UpdateLayoutTree: median 511.04ms -> 449.60ms, a drop of 61.44ms
    (~12%), same direction as predicted, but SMALLER than this metric's
    own ~81ms baseline noise floor, and the two 3-run sets overlap (not a
    clean separation): baseline range [470.79, 552.10] vs variant range
    [437.53, 505.25] -- variant run1 (505.25) sits inside baseline's own
    range, above baseline run1 (470.79). Cannot call this movement beyond
    noise on all three runs.
  UpdateLayoutTree temporal distribution: UNCHANGED. Same two-cluster,
    ~800-1000ms-wide-burst shape, same elevated-bucket counts (8-9 both
    conditions), in every one of the 6 runs. This is the specific,
    falsifiable mechanism the claim proposed (recalc becomes scoped/local,
    "concentrate almost entirely at the two frames") and it did not occur.
  framesOver33ms: median 45 -> 43, a 2-frame drop, at/near the task's own
    1-frame calibrated noise floor and smaller than either condition's own
    within-condition spread here (2 and 4 frames respectively) -- not a
    confident signal either way.
  framesOver50ms: median 0 -> 2, moved in the WRONG direction. Baseline
    max across 3 runs is 1; two of three variant runs (2, 2) sit strictly
    above that. Small in absolute terms (2 frames out of ~280-300) but
    consistent and in the opposite direction from "smoother."
  p95/intervalMsMax: essentially flat (33.4ms both conditions); maxMs hit
    66.6-66.7ms in 2 of 3 variant runs vs 1 of 3 baseline runs -- mildly
    more frequent long frames under the neutralizer, not fewer.
  World switches: 2 -> 2 on all 3 runs, identical color sequence in every
    run of both conditions. Unlike T3's block neutralizer, this one does
    not disturb the switch mechanism itself -- a genuine point in its
    favor even though it does not fix the jank.
  Secondary, non-primary metrics (disclosed, not part of the named claim):
  Paint and EventDispatch BOTH show a full run-level separation in the
  predicted direction -- baseline Paint [315.80, 324.25, 347.10] vs
  variant Paint [261.53, 268.13, 271.42] (every variant run below every
  baseline run); baseline EventDispatch [320.13, 343.30, 361.35] vs
  variant EventDispatch [286.16, 292.33, 316.51] (every variant run below
  every baseline run, 316.51 < 320.13). These are real, clean, disclosed
  movements -- but they are not the metric the claim named
  (UpdateLayoutTree), and do not by themselves establish the claim's
  specific "page-wide recalc becomes scoped" mechanism, which the
  distribution data above directly contradicts. FunctionCall shows no
  consistent pattern (baseline [377.18, 407.60, 425.68] vs variant
  [390.48, 397.69, 428.64] -- ranges overlap almost completely).

VERDICT: not_causal.
The claim is compound and specific: UpdateLayoutTree total should drop AND
its per-frame distribution should concentrate from "recurring broadly"
down to "almost entirely two isolated frames," because the fix scopes a
currently page-wide recalc to the sections that changed. On the
distribution half -- the half that most directly tests the proposed
mechanism -- the evidence is clean, symmetric, and fully reproduced across
all 6 runs: baseline and variant show the identical two-cluster,
multi-hundred-ms-wide burst shape, with the same elevated-bucket counts
(8-9 either condition) and no consistent per-event improvement (largest
single-event durations do not separate between conditions). The
neutralizer did not localize or narrow the recalc in the way the
mechanism requires. On the total-ms half, the median did move 61ms in the
predicted direction, but that movement is smaller than this metric's own
~81ms baseline noise floor (computed the same way T1's noise floor was
computed, since the task gave no explicit calibration for this metric),
and the two 3-run sets overlap rather than cleanly separate. A secondary
jank indicator (framesOver50ms) moved mildly in the WRONG direction
(0->2 median, 2 of 3 variant runs above baseline's max), and the primary
frame-smoothness metrics the operator actually feels (p95, framesOver33ms)
are flat within noise. Per the task's own verdict rule ("causal only if
the variant moves the metric beyond the noise floor in the predicted
direction on all three runs"), this does not qualify: one half of the
compound metric does not move at all, the other half's movement does not
clear its own noise floor, and no frame-smoothness metric shows a clean
beyond-noise improvement. This does not mean content-visibility:auto had
zero effect on the page -- Paint and EventDispatch totals both showed
full-separation drops -- but those are not the mechanism or the metric the
claim staked itself on, and are disclosed here as a secondary finding for
a possible future, more narrowly-framed experiment, not as grounds to
upgrade this verdict. One reassuring, disclosed side-finding: the
neutralizer did not break the world-switch mechanism itself (2 switches,
identical colors, identical order, all 6 runs, zero console errors) --
whatever is causing the reported scroll jank and "gkitchy" color
transition, it is not something this specific neutralizer fixes, but it
also is not something this specific neutralizer breaks further.
