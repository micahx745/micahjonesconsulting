## Outline

### Why are people visiting without signing up?

**Answer:** Separate three events: a qualified visit, a first useful result, and a request to continue. Watch five sessions and interview five people who stopped. Do not add features until you know which step loses them.

**Mechanism:** A small funnel turns “no traction” into a specific problem with the audience, promise, onboarding, or product.

**Tools:** PostHog (VERIFY), Microsoft Clarity (VERIFY), Google Analytics (VERIFY).

### Who should I ask to become an early user?

**Answer:** Choose one narrow group already trying to solve the problem. List 25 people and ask for 15 minutes to see how they handle it today. Invite only the people whose current process matches your product.

**Mechanism:** Existing behavior reveals urgency, current alternatives, and the words people use when the problem is real.

**Tools:** LinkedIn (VERIFY), Reddit (VERIFY), Google Sheets (VERIFY).

### What should my landing page say?

**Answer:** Lead with the buyer’s problem, the promised outcome, and who the product serves. Put the technical story lower on the page. Offer one next step: try a narrow task, book a call, or pay.

**Mechanism:** A concrete promise lets readers compare the product with the process they already tolerate.

**Tools:** Google Docs (VERIFY), Carrd (VERIFY), Webflow (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** At Guardicore, I “ran the research that found what those buyers were actually signing for” and moved the story to east-west visibility.

### Where should I look for my first users?

**Answer:** Go where those 25 people already discuss the problem. Answer existing questions and publish one useful teardown or checklist. Stay with one channel for two weeks before adding another.

**Mechanism:** Repeated participation creates familiarity while the replies expose questions your landing page has not answered.

**Tools:** Reddit (VERIFY), LinkedIn (VERIFY), Indie Hackers (VERIFY), GitHub Discussions (VERIFY).

### Should I charge less to attract early users?

**Answer:** Do not use a low price to cover unclear value. Ask for payment once you can deliver one useful outcome. Offer direct onboarding to remove uncertainty without weakening the price.

**Mechanism:** Payment tests urgency, while direct onboarding exposes objections and unfinished parts of the product.

**Tools:** Stripe Payment Links (VERIFY), Calendly (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** My company ORDANI “has active paying users today, it is in beta, and a public release is coming.”

### How do I know what to change next?

**Answer:** Run a seven-day test with one audience, one promise, and one next step. Track replies, calls, completed first outcomes, and payments. Keep the product fixed while testing the message, then reverse the test.

**Mechanism:** Changing one variable at a time tells you whether the problem sits in the product, message, audience, or channel.

**Tools:** Google Sheets (VERIFY), Notion (VERIFY), PostHog (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** For one birth worker, “Bookings went from one to three a month to five to ten.” I repositioned the practice and rebuilt how clients found and booked her.
