1) **KILLS IT? Almost.** The voice works and the outreach is actionable, but it skips turning interviewees into users and presents several guesses as rules.

2) **Five fixes:**

- “That request gets replies because it costs the other person nothing and flatters them a little.”  
  Fifteen minutes and screen access cost something. Fix: “Keep the request specific and small; explain what they might get from helping.”

- “If you cannot get to ten, you do not have a segment yet, you have a feature.”  
  Punchy but false. Fix: “If you cannot name ten, narrow the group or find a better place to reach them.”

- “Fewer than three means the list is wrong or the first line is.”  
  Unsupported diagnosis; the preceding reply benchmark is unsupported too. Fix: “Low replies can reflect targeting, timing, trust or the request itself. Test one change before judging demand.”

- “Free tells you that free was easy to accept, and nothing else.”  
  Fix: “Free signups do not prove willingness to pay; repeat use can still prove usefulness.”

- “Ten visits and zero signups is a sample of ten, not a conversion rate, so count people you can name.”  
  Fix: “Zero signups from ten visits is too little evidence to judge conversion.”

3) **Wrong or risky advice:** This assumes a paid workflow tool; manual work and immediate charging are not universal filters. Count completed payments and repeat use, not verbal yeses. The tooling detour invites procrastination: [Umami tracks more than visits](https://docs.umami.is/docs), and [PostHog self-hosting is unsupported](https://posthog.com/docs/self-host).

4) **Best search-intent improvement:** Add **“How do I turn an interview into a first user?”** Explain the missing handoff: offer a guided trial on their next task, agree on success, then check whether they return unaided.
