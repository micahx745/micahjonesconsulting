## Lede

For an AI-built product with one signup, I start where buyers already gather and answer their question before asking for a signup. Pick one channel, watch the language people use, and post a specific fix. If one user signed up, ask that person what almost kept them away. Then make the next person’s first five minutes smaller and clearer.

## Outline

### Why did only one user sign up?

**Answer:** The page usually names the product instead of the problem. Pull the exact pain phrases from public posts and put them above the fold. Remove every line that asks for belief before it gives proof.

**Mechanism:** When the first sentence matches the thought in a visitor’s head, they stay long enough to read the next step.

**Tools:** Reddit search (VERIFY), Google Search Console (VERIFY), Plausible (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** “I find what your buyers are actually paying for, then build the system that sells exactly that.”

### Where do early users actually hang out?

**Answer:** Pick one community where your future user already asks about the problem. Search the last month of questions and answer three posts with a useful process, not a link. Then post your own result only when the community allows it.

**Mechanism:** People click a profile after they receive a specific answer. The product becomes the next step, not the opening pitch.

**Tools:** Reddit (VERIFY), Indie Hackers (VERIFY), Hacker News (VERIFY), X search (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** None fits here.

---

### What do I say when I post or email?

**Answer:** Lead with the problem, the expensive current workaround, and a small fix. Show the output or the exact steps, and mention your product only as the shortcut after the fix. Use words the buyer already typed, not your internal name for the feature.

**Mechanism:** Useful advice proves competence in seconds. A specific fix creates a reason to see how the product does the same job faster.

**Tools:** ChatGPT (VERIFY), Claude (VERIFY), Reddit search (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** “I ran the research that found what those buyers were actually signing for, moved the story from honeypots to east-west visibility, and sat in the deals.”

---

### Should I price low to get users or high to filter serious buyers?

**Answer:** Do not stay free unless you need onboarding reps. Pick a low paid price that covers your time and makes the objection explicit. If a buyer goes quiet after the price, you learned something free users cannot teach.

**Mechanism:** A paid user is evidence the problem is worth money. A free user is only evidence that the free part was easy.

**Tools:** Stripe Payment Links (VERIFY), Gumroad (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** None fits here.

---

### What do I fix on the landing page before I spend more time building?

**Answer:** Keep one headline that says who it is for and what changes, one next step, and one proof point. Remove anything that requires a tour or a call to understand. Show the before state and the after state in an image or two.

**Mechanism:** A first visit decides quickly on a phone. The page must carry the full argument without a person walking beside it.

**Tools:** Google PageSpeed Insights (VERIFY), Screaming Frog SEO Spider (VERIFY), Plausible (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** “I go through your build, your production, or your positioning top to bottom.”

---

### How do I turn one user into ten?

**Answer:** Interview the one user for the words they used before switching. Keep a short case note with the before, the trigger, and the result. Make a narrow ask to two people who already have the same problem.

**Mechanism:** A referral works when the story is specific and the ask is narrow. A broad “share this” request does not travel.

**Tools:** Cal.com (VERIFY), Notion (VERIFY).

**Proof point:** None fits here.

---

### When should I pay for an outside set of eyes?

**Answer:** When one or two people sign up and the next fix is not obvious, a paid audit is cheaper than another silent month. You get a memo that names what is broken and what to fix first. Then you run the first item the morning it lands.

**Mechanism:** The block is usually a positioning or product decision you have stopped seeing because you built it. An outside review shortens the correction loop.

**Tools:** None.
