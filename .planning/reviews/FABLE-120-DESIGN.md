# FABLE-120-DESIGN: /work index and the case-study template

Fable 5.1 standing in for Astra, 2026-09-16. One look, fifteen tool calls. Inputs: the two Pass-120
drafts, DESIGN_BAR R1-R20, brand.json, today's /work and Guardicore captures at 1440, the two
contact sheets, and four references at full length (d-danmall-spine, d-harpalsingh-work,
a-instrument-pagerduty, c-nytimes-snowfall). The other ten I judged from the contact sheets and
the teardown JSON. Where the JSON and a capture disagreed I went with the capture.

What today's captures show, before the references say anything:

- /work opens on a dark band with `$14M`, a dek, the Tel Aviv photograph, and a mono line that
  prints `2018-2021`. Below it, six entries on paper, each carrying a number, a title, a dek, a
  role line with tenure years, a read link, and three display-size stats with mono captions.
  That is nine data points per entry against R11's four, and the stats restate the dek.
- The Guardicore study opens on four words (`PIPELINE. RESEARCH. NARRATIVE. ACQUIRED.`) that
  fill the first 1440 fold. The client, the result and the photograph are one scroll down. A
  sticky rail prints `Year 2018-2021`. A grey `PROTECTED BY NDA` box stands where an image
  would go. The reading column is narrow bone on obsidian for the whole page.

Every reference in the set, all four classes, puts the subject in the first fold. None of them
opens on a word stack.

## 1. What the set teaches

### Holds across all four classes

**The first fold names the thing.** Collins puts `Mailchimp` at 72px with the yellow lockup;
Instrument makes the wordmark the hero at 184px; Koto's hero is the amazon logotype; Harvey's
banner leads with `$550M`; Spine puts the story's title and dek in one dark card; Harpal's entries
are `Client, outcome in one line` as H2s; UXGen leads its card with `3.2% to 8.5%`. The only
references that do not name the subject above the fold at 1440 are Instacart (a phone mockup
stands in for it) and Works in Progress (an illustration does), and both are budget choices, not
method. Today's study hero is the outlier in the whole set.

**Proof is a number with its meaning beside it, never a bare number.** Instacart's tiles
(`1,100+ retail banners`), Brex's band (`756 hours`, `71%`), Sky's chips (`3.60% APY`), UXGen's
strip (`3.2% to 8.5%`, `9 minutes`). The tile is not the mechanism; the caption is. Today's
/work stats already do this. The study template does not: `$14M / $1.2M / 2021` have mono captions
but sit as a row apart from the story.

**Proof types are stacked within inches of each other.** PagerDuty puts the 37% sentence, a
titled quote and the award row in one block. Harvey puts the Repsol video, the stat grid and the
compliance badges together. April Dunford puts headshot, name and title on each quote. The reader
is not made to collect proof across the page.

**Reading pages read on light ground.** Snow Fall, Pudding, Works in Progress, Harpal, Collins,
Dunford: every page built to be read for more than a minute is light. The dark references are
either image walls (Koto, 153 photographs), campaign pages (PagerDuty's alternating Volt and
black, with illustration on every black section), a single essay with nothing else on it (Spine),
or a dark-mode SaaS card (UXGen). Nobody sets 3,000 words of prose on black without imagery to
break it. That is what the Guardicore page does today, and what three anonymous studies would do
on the same template.

**Anonymity is stated, then ignored.** Spine never names the client and states an exact fee
schedule, quotes the emails verbatim and reads as the most specific page in the set. Harpal's
`Investment Bank` entry names the NDA in one sentence and then gets the same Role / Engagement /
Location block as the named cases. UXGen substitutes a category label (`CLIENT: SERIES B MARTECH
SAAS`) plus Product / Users / Problem / Role bullets. None of them puts a grey box where a
screenshot would have been.

**The page ends with one route onward, not a shelf.** Spine's `Read this next` is one post.
Harpal's sections end in one `Enquire` line. Where the page ends on a card shelf (Instacart,
Instrument, Collins), the cards are the studio's other clients, which is a portfolio move this
site's rules already refuse.

### Where the classes disagree

**Studios (class A) sell the wall; solo operators (class D) sell the record.** Koto's page lands
because it has 153 images of Amazon; Instacart's because of four video loops and a prop shoot;
PagerDuty's because of a custom illustration system and an OOH campaign. None of that is method.
Spine, Harpal and Dunford land on specificity: a fee, a role block, a price band. This site is
class D with two real photographs, so the class D mechanisms transfer and the class A ones mostly
do not.

**Product sites (class B) put the ask in the hero; everyone else defers it.** Brex captures an
email at the fold; Harvey's `Request a Demo` is in the nav, hero and close; UXGen repeats `Book an
Audit` three times. Studios and editorial carry no ask at all. Class D sits between: Dunford's
sticky tab is selling pressure (R17 fails it); Harpal's single plain `Enquire` line per section is
the right register for this site. The drafts already do it: each study closes into its /services
block, once.

**Editorial (class C) alternates column and chapter break; everyone else stacks sections.**
Snow Fall's narrow column punctuated by full-bleed photographs is the one rhythm in the set that
gives a real photograph a job beyond decoration. It only works where a photograph exists
(Guardicore: one, ORDANI: one). It is still worth taking for those two.

**On hero video the set is unanimous in a way the operator should hear.** Zero of fourteen
autoplay a video in the hero. Instacart and Koto loop muted clips lower down, as budget
decoration. Snow Fall and Harvey gate video behind a poster and a click. Nobody in the set uses
generated motion of a real person.

## 2. Three directions

All three share what the content and the constitution have already fixed, so I state it once:

- Fonts: Bricolage Grotesque for the figure and titles, Hanken Grotesk for everything read,
  JetBrains Mono only for the at-a-glance keys, the step numerals, the record block's role and
  year columns, and the `Name protected` label.
- The type scale, five active sizes per page (R2). At 1440: **112** (the result figure), **56**
  (the search title as h1), **36** (section h2 and the `Results` value in at-a-glance), **18**
  (body, 1.6 line-height, 68ch column), **13** (mono labels, +0.06em tracking). Adjacent ratios
  2.0, 1.56, 2.0, 1.38. Step headings and buyer questions are Hanken 18 at weight 600, size shared
  with body and separated by weight, as the draft's bold lead-ins already read. At 390: **64 / 36
  / 26 / 17 / 12**, ratios 1.78, 1.38, 1.53, 1.42. The 112 figure is 6.2x body; the bar asks 4x.
- Colour: copper only, copper-deep for any link inside body copy on paper (Pitfall B1), sage
  replacing copper on /work/ordani only. No second accent, no gradient, no tint other than
  `theater-surface` and `rule-*`.
- Index entries at four data points (R11): context label (mono), one figure-bearing line
  (Bricolage 36), one line of what he did (Hanken 18), the service it routes to (mono). No stat
  trios, no `read the case study` link duplicated under an already-linked entry, no years of any
  kind.
- No sticky rail on studies. Koto's fixed TOC serves a 39,000px page; these run 3,500 to 6,000px
  and the rail today exists mostly to print `Role / Scope / Year`, which the at-a-glance block
  now carries in the first fold.
- The grey `Protected by NDA` boxes are removed in all three. A box that poses as an image says
  "an image is missing here". The anonymous studies stand on a typographic exhibit instead (the
  RFP worked example, `One requirement, start to finish`, set as a two-column block on a surface
  tint with mono labels `The request / What the engine did`), the `What the replay found`
  section as the technical block, and the at-a-glance block carrying `Name protected` as a plain
  value where the client name would sit (Harpal's sentence, UXGen's label).
- The record block, in all three: h2 `Also on the record`, the one-line count, four rows ordered
  by exit year. Company in Bricolage 36 (Guardicore linked to its study), role and what happened
  in mono 13, the one-line description in Hanken 18 beneath. A table at 1440 (company | role |
  what happened, description spanning beneath), stacked rows at 390 with the mono pair on one
  line. No CTA in the block. The 301s from the two retired slugs land on `#record`.
- Motion: the foyer-to-theater dim stays. The TitleCard is re-cast, not removed, in A and B (see
  each), removed in C. Nothing loops. Index entries get an underline on hover, no lift.

## 3. Recommendation

**Direction B.** It is the only one of the three that passes both tests the set imposes at once:
the first fold names the client and the result on a ground with weight, and the long read sits on
paper where every reading reference in the set puts it. It keeps the foyer-to-theater dim as the
signature and gives the TitleCard's cadence an honest home as a 600ms entrance on a hero that
finally says who and how much. It makes /work and the five studies one reading system, which is
what "individual pages need to all have same design" means in practice: the same column, the same
five sizes, the same at-a-glance block, the same close. And it solves the anonymous-study problem
without a fake image: on obsidian, a study with no photograph is a hole; on paper after a dark
curtain, it is a page. Direction A keeps more of today and inherits today's weakness three times
over. Direction C is honest but throws away the one visual identity the site has. B costs one
build mechanism (a scoped surface override inside a theater route) and one ruling on the
TitleCard, both listed in section 7.

## 4. The hero video

The file: 4.04s, 1440x1440, an image-to-video generation from the real Tel Aviv photograph. His
mouth and head move; colleagues are in frame and move too.

**Against R12.** R12 says every image is a real artifact and names AI-generated imagery as a
fail. Frame 0 is the photograph. Every frame after it is synthesized: the mouth, the tilt, the
colleagues' movement are the model's invention, not a record of the room. It is AI-generated
imagery derived from a real artifact, and on a site whose whole argument is that its numbers
and photographs are real, the first moving thing a buyer sees would be the one thing that is not.
A buyer who notices (and the 2026 buyer notices) learns the site animates reality. That is the
wrong first lesson for a positioning consultant. **Fails R12, and R12 is a core criterion.**

**Against R15.** As a loop it is an idle loop, banned outright. Played once, it ends on a
synthesized frame, not the photograph.

**Against LCP.** A 1440x1440 mp4 in the first fold on mobile competes with a 1.8s budget the
page has to hit with the photograph already there. Chrome scores a video's poster or first frame
as the LCP candidate; the safe pattern in the set is Snow Fall's (poster image, click to play),
which then costs a click for four seconds of motion.

**Consent.** The still is published, so the colleagues' presence in it is a settled matter. The
video is a different act: it puts behaviour into their bodies that they did not perform. Even
with a release for the photograph, no one consented to being animated.

**Against the references.** None of the fourteen autoplays hero video; none uses generated
motion of a person; the two that loop clips at all (a-wolffolins-instacart, a-koto-amazon) are
budget decoration lower on the page.

**Options, honestly:**

1. **The photograph, at hero scale, with its real caption. No video.** Passes everything, costs
   nothing, and the still already does what he likes about the clip: it shows him working with
   people. This is the base state whatever else happens.
2. **Give the photograph the motion through the signature that already exists.** Name the
   photograph as the shared element of the foyer-to-theater view transition, so it travels from
   the /work hero into the Guardicore curtain as the visitor enters the study. That is 600ms of
   real, once-only motion on the real artifact, inside the one signature, at no LCP cost. It keeps
   the operator's idea (the picture becomes cinematic) inside the rules. **This is the one I
   recommend, on top of option 1.**
3. **Real footage, if any exists.** A few seconds of actual phone video from any working session
   or talk, muted, poster = the still, plays once on load and holds its last real frame, ≤3s,
   skipped under reduced motion and on save-data; or click-to-play behind the poster (Snow Fall).
   Passes R12 and R15. Needs a yes from anyone identifiable. It is an operator question whether
   such footage exists; the harness should not manufacture the need.
4. **Use the generated clip off the site.** A LinkedIn post, a deck, a message, where the
   medium does not carry the site's claim to real artifacts. The site is the wrong surface for it.
5. **Rejected:** any Ken Burns drift or slow zoom on the still. It is the parallax family, a
   second motion, and the references that drift are the ones with 3D token blobs (b-clay-sky).

**Ruling:** the generated clip does not ship on the site in any form. Ship option 1 now, build
option 2 into the Pass-120 brief, and leave option 3 open for the day real footage exists.

## 7. What the build brief must specify that a mockup cannot settle

- **The surface override.** How a `data-mode="theater"` route carries a paper body region:
  a `data-surface="paper"` attribute on the body wrapper with the token remap in globals.css,
  and the rule that only the study template may use it. Contrast re-checked on both grounds
  (copper-deep for links on paper, copper on obsidian).
- **The view transition's shared element.** The `ViewTransition` name for the photograph on
  /work and on the Guardicore curtain (React 19 import, not Next), what happens when the study
  has no photograph (no shared element, plain dim), and the reduced-motion kill switch.
- **The TitleCard settle.** Exact timings (≤600ms total, per-line offset, ease-out, transform and
  opacity only), once per load, reserved height so CLS stays under 0.05, the finished frame as
  the no-JS and reduced-motion render, GSAP still quarantined to the one file, and the brand.json
  `motion.signature` entry rewritten to describe it. `titleCardWords` removed from the frontmatter
  schema and the Zod validator.
- **Frontmatter for the new spine.** `client` (string or `Name protected`), `atAGlance` rows,
  `results` value, `service` slug for the close, `hero` optional; `year` never rendered; the
  record block as data in `content/` with the four rows and one description each; CDC figures
  through `content/citations.ts` (Pitfall E2).
- **LCP at 390 with the photograph as the candidate.** Preload, `sizes`, an AVIF at ≤120KB for
  the 800w slot, and the measured number reported, not asserted.
- **Verification commands with expected output.** `curl -s` greps proving no tenure year renders
  on /work or any study (`grep -c '20[0-9][0-9]-20[0-9][0-9]'` returns 0), the two 301s resolve
  to `/work#record`, the banned-list grep on every new string, axe on the definition list and the
  record table, and the five active sizes measured per page.
- **The typographic exhibit's markup.** The RFP worked example as a two-column block with mono
  labels, how it stacks at 390, and that it is the only surface-tinted block type on anonymous
  studies, so three studies do not each invent one.
- **The step numeral column.** Its width at 1440, its collapse above the heading at 390, and the
  copper (or sage) numeral's contrast on paper.
- **What the `Next` entry links to.** The order of the five, the wrap from the last to the first
  or to /work, and that the entry carries four data points and nothing else.
