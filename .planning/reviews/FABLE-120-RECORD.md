# FABLE-120: the record, judged as one body of work

Pass-120 judge look, 2026-09-15. Read-only. Rules on the seven studies as a record, not as
prose; Astra holds the craft pass.

## Verdict

Read together, the seven say "a salesman who later learned to code, with two exits he stood
near." That is not what the record proves. What it proves, five times, is one method: he goes
to the sales data and the buyers before he touches the pitch or the product, finds what people
are actually paying for, then builds and runs the thing that sells it. The drafts hide this by
filing each study under one of three service blocks, so the site reads as three consultants
sharing a surname. The record is publishable once the lead is checkable, the thin two are
demoted to rows, one count is killed, and the anonymous-author drift is reconciled across
surfaces. It is not publishable with the bracketed tags open on the RFP and birth-worker
pages; those are the claims a buyer asks about on the call.

## Rulings

**1. What the record says he is.** The most valuable true claim: "I find what your buyers
are actually paying for, then build the system that sells it, and the record shows it in
money." Reason: every study supports that sentence and no agency or lone engineer can say it.
- Guardicore carries the diagnosis half at enterprise scale: buyers signing for visibility
  while the deck sold honeypots.
- The RFP engine carries both halves in one engagement. /services already says he repositioned
  the author toward contract buyers; the draft then shows him building the software that won
  $3M. The draft cut the positioning half. Put it back as step 00; it is the study's value.
- The content engine and Ordani carry the build half; the birth worker carries the method at
  its smallest scale.
- Disagreement with the drafts: each closes into one service block. Every close names the
  method first and the block second.

**2. Order and the lead.**
- 01 Guardicore. The only study a stranger can verify without asking him: named client, named
  acquirer, a photograph of him in the room. An anonymous $3M cannot open a record; the first
  question is "says who."
- 02 RFP engine. The most recent money, and proof the Guardicore skill is still for sale.
- 03 Ordani. Proves the build claim with his own capital; in-flight, so never the lead.
- 04 Content engine. The build half with a revenue mechanism; below the RFP because reach is
  not revenue.
- 05 Birth worker. The only pure consulting-era positioning receipt; 30% of a solo practice is
  a small absolute, so it closes the pages.
- 06, 07 Postmates and Neuton as rows (ruling 4).

**3. What is missing.** One question each.
- $20M+ since 2013 against a consulting record that starts in 2024: "Which two or three
  engagements between 2013 and 2023 produced most of the $20M, and can one be told
  anonymously?" The largest gap on the site.
- Guardicore: "Is $14M revenue you closed, revenue you originated, or the company's revenue in
  your years? One word, and the page uses that word."
- RFP engine: "What did the author sell before you pointed them at contract buyers, and what
  changed in the offer?" (the missing step 00).
- Content engine: "Which stream was the one, and did total revenue rise, or only the count of
  streams?" And: "290,000 of what: views, impressions, or accounts reached?"
- Ordani: "Is a paying-practice count ruled out for good, or only until public release?"
- Birth worker: the draft's three. Period, baseline, year.
- Postmates: "How many months, and what is the one deliverable you would sign?"
- Neuton: "Were you paid by Neuton, for how long, and was the deliverable a plan, a launch, or
  first customers?"
- SurveyMonkey is a /services receipt with no entry on /work: "Row or page?"

**4. The thin two.** Rows, not pages. Reason: a page that cannot say what he did, for how
long, or what changed says "I was near an exit," and says it at hero scale. Under the standing
rulings Postmates has two sentences and Neuton has one; a page amplifies thinness, a row
states the fact at its true weight. Build: a ruled block at the foot of /work, "Also on the
record," two rows, at most four data points each per R11 (company, role, years, what happened
after), no CTA. 301 both slugs to /work#record. The Postmates and Neuton drafts are not
written up.

**5. The one claim to kill.** "One of four companies I worked inside that reached an exit,"
in both thin drafts and implied wherever the four are counted. Reason: a buyer with money
knows a part-year analyst role in 2020 and a launch assist five years before an undisclosed
acquisition are not exits he reached; the count restores the causal link the ledger forbids,
by arithmetic instead of prose. Replacement: the two dated rows from ruling 4, so the reader
counts, and the only exit count on the site stays /about's "two carried my name on the cap
table," which is ledgered and true.

Two demotions, not kills:
- "36×" leaves every headline: page title, /work metadata, /services receipt. A multiplier on
  the softest metric in marketing. The 8,000 and 290,000 stay, with their unit; "one income
  stream became four" becomes the lead figure.
- "Award-winning" leaves the RFP title, description and dek. R16 names it as hype vocabulary;
  "an author whose books are taught in government bodies" says more and passes.

**6. SEO versus taste.**
- Free: title tags, meta descriptions, the H1, the dek, alt text, JSON-LD. No reader is
  cheapened by a keyword there. Write these for search without apology.
- Cheapens: the "Questions buyers ask" blocks when the question is a glossary entry. "What is
  east-west traffic?" is a crawler's question; "Can one person build a HIPAA product?" is a
  buyer's. Keep an FAQ only where he was asked it on a call. Also cheapens: search-term
  phrases in H2s, and the "Search terms" lists steering the openers.
- For the blog: each study ends by naming the general problem it is one instance of (pitch
  versus contract; notebook versus production; a promise wider than the business), so a post
  on that problem has a study to cite and the study links back. The studies point at money;
  the blog points at method, and the glossary answers move there.

**7. Three page-level answers the drafts asked for.**
- Content engine stays under Product building. Reason: the buyer bought a strategy and a
  system, not a model.
- The birth worker's close points at the Positioning block unchanged; no second pain line for
  practices. Reason: the page kicker is "For companies"; the study is proof of method, not an
  invitation to a second market. Let it say the method is the same at enterprise scale and
  link to Guardicore in-line.
- Derived values move with the 2026-09-15 ruling that the two anonymous clients are different
  people. Today LESSONS #3 says "same person," /services calls the content-engine client "an
  industry author," and the /work metadata says "36x reach for an author"; the draft says
  "social activist." One ruling, four surfaces, one sweep, before either page ships.
