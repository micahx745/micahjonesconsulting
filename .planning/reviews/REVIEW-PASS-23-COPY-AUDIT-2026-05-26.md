# Pass-23 copy audit — REVIEW

## TL;DR

- **Biggest take:** The Pass-23 rewrites landed. The pain-point-led service descriptions and the HR-equity case study are the strongest copy on the site now — but the home page still carries five or six sentences that describe what you ARE instead of naming a pain the buyer FEELS, and those sentences drag the whole page toward portfolio-mode.
- **Sharpest cut:** The Ordani case study opening paragraph repeats the dek verbatim. Cut the entire first body paragraph — the reader already read it two inches above.
- **Protect this:** The `/services` footer CTA ("Every engagement starts with a 30-minute call to name the shape and the fit. No deck, no sales pitch — just whether the work maps to what you actually need.") is the best closer on the entire site. Do not touch it.

## Verdict per surface

### `/` (home) — overall grade: B

The home page is two pages stitched together: a strong pain-led middle (services row, operating principles, Ordani section) and a weaker top-and-bottom that still leans on credential narration. The Pass-23 rewrites (services row, How I Work) elevated the middle of the page significantly. The hero, revenue section, and shipped grid haven't caught up.

**What works:** The services row descriptions are the best single improvement from Pass-23. `"You built it. Enterprise teams still aren't buying. The gap is positioning, not features."` — that sentence names a pain the buyer already feels, in their own internal language. Protect it.

**Punch list:**

1. **"Strategy and software, shipped by the same pair of hands. Enterprise GTM and *a product in private beta with fourteen practices.*"**
   - Problem: The first sentence is strong. The second sentence is a credential, not a pain. "Enterprise GTM and a product in private beta" describes what you do, not why a buyer should care. The italicized clause feels like a footnote grafted onto a headline.
   - Rewrite: `Strategy and software, shipped by the same pair of hands. Enterprise GTM for founders who built the product but can't close the enterprise sale.`

2. **"in client revenue across a decade. Two enterprise exits. Trillions in digital assets behind the deployments."**
   - Problem: "Two enterprise exits" is a credential-stacking sentence between two stronger ones. The reader already sees the exits detailed below. It's a preview of a preview.
   - Rewrite: `in client revenue across a decade. Trillions in digital assets behind the deployments.` (Cut the middle sentence; the exits earn their own section immediately below.)

3. **"I work where AI prototypes die: the production gap. Eval, orchestration, the failure modes you haven't named yet."**
   - Problem: This is good but "the failure modes you haven't named yet" is a claim about the buyer's ignorance. Reads slightly condescending at display scale on a home page. In a sales call it would land. In print it reads like a dare.
   - Rewrite: `I work where AI prototypes die: the production gap. Eval, orchestration, the failure modes between demo and deployment.`

4. **"Every engagement ships a named artifact in month one. No decks. No discovery debt."**
   - Problem: None. This is among the best lines on the site. Leave it.

5. **"I've shipped product deployed at TD Bank, Deutsche Bank, NIH, Peoples Natural Gas. I know what enterprise procurement actually pays for."**
   - Problem: The second sentence ("I know what enterprise procurement actually pays for") is a claim. The first sentence already proves it. Let the receipts do the work.
   - Rewrite: `I've shipped product deployed at TD Bank, Deutsche Bank, NIH, Peoples Natural Gas.` (Full stop. The customer names carry the weight without the follow-up claim.)

6. **"I've been on the inside of two exits — one acquisition, one IPO. I know what acquisition-ready looks like."**
   - Problem: Same pattern as #5 — strong evidence sentence followed by a weaker claim sentence. "I know what acquisition-ready looks like" is a conclusion the buyer should draw, not one you should announce.
   - Rewrite: `I've been on the inside of two exits — one acquisition, one IPO.` (Full stop. Or, if you want a second beat: `I've been on the inside of two exits — one acquisition, one IPO. That shapes how I build.`)

7. **"Two platforms acquired and public. A content engine that wins inbound. Frontier AI in production for founders."**
   - Problem: This is the Shipped dek. Three tight sentences, all working. Leave it.

8. **"Algorithm strategy, content engine, and bespoke product work for an industry-authority author."**
   - Problem: "Industry-authority author" is a category placeholder. The home-page card for this engagement doesn't name the stakes or the outcome. Compare to the Guardicore card, which names deal-size movement and an acquisition. This card names deliverables (algorithm strategy, content engine) but not results.
   - Rewrite: `Algorithm strategy and content engine for an industry author. Monthly reach: 8K → 290K. RFP-to-close rate doubled.`

9. **"The engineering between the model and the user — eval infrastructure, RAG, agent orchestration, prompt-deployment pipelines. Specifics under NDA. Available for new engagements."**
   - Problem: "Available for new engagements" is a staffing-agency phrase. It breaks the operator register. The NDA disclosure is necessary; the availability announcement is not.
   - Rewrite: `The engineering between the model and the user — eval infrastructure, RAG, agent orchestration, prompt-deployment pipelines. Specifics under NDA.`

10. **"NAME THE PROBLEM →"**
    - Problem: None. Strong CTA. Different from every "Let's talk" footer. Keep.

**Cuts:**
- Revenue dek: cut "Two enterprise exits." — redundant with the section immediately below.
- Operating principles #5 claim sentence: cut "I know what enterprise procurement actually pays for."
- Operating principles #6 claim sentence: cut "I know what acquisition-ready looks like." (or soften to "That shapes how I build.")
- Shipped card 3: cut "Available for new engagements."

**What works:** `"I'm a Black founder, a father, and someone who would rather show you a working thing than a slide about a working thing."` — That sentence does three things at once: it names identity without performing it, it names a value (building > presenting), and it implies a buyer filter (if you want slides, I'm not your person). Excellent line.

**Punch list:**

1. **"I build the things I used to ask other people to build."**
   - Problem: Strong H1. Works as an opening. Leave it.

2. **"The consulting half means a small number of operators every quarter: HR consultants, nonprofit leaders, birth workers, creators."**
   - Problem: The client list here (HR consultants, nonprofit leaders, birth workers, creators) doesn't match the target buyer described in the brief (Series A-C founders shipping AI-native software, B2B SaaS operators). This page describes a different client base than the one the home page is selling to. If the about page is supposed to serve the same buyer as the home page, this sentence needs to name that buyer.
   - Rewrite: `The consulting half means a small number of engagements every quarter — founders shipping AI-native software, B2B operators preparing for enterprise sales or acquisition, and the occasional industry author who needs infrastructure, not an agency.`

3. **"the people who keep Black women alive in childbirth"**
   - Problem: This is the strongest single phrase on the about page. It names the stakes of Ordani without any abstraction. Protect it.

4. **"Most of my best work happens at night, after the house goes quiet."**
   - Problem: None. This appears twice — once in the body paragraph, once in the sidebar block ("Lives in Oakland with his family. Father of two. Builds at night, after the house goes quiet."). The repetition is fine because the two contexts are different (narrative vs. sidebar bio). Leave both.

5. **"Owns more receipts than slides."**
   - Problem: Slightly cute. "Receipts" has been internet-vernacular for long enough that it reads more Twitter than editorial. But it echoes the "show the receipts" principle from the numbered list below, so it holds together. Borderline — leave it but watch for fatigue if "receipts" appears too many more times on the site.

**Cuts:**
- None. This page is tight.

The strongest page on the site. The Pass-23 description rewrites elevated all three service entries from category descriptions to pain-led openers. The tier grid is detailed and specific. The footer CTA is the single best closing paragraph on the site.

**What works:** `"You built it. Enterprise teams still aren't buying. The gap is positioning, not features."` — Best opening line across all three services. It names a pain the buyer has already felt, in two sentences, without mentioning the operator at all.

**Punch list:**

1. **"Most AI ideas die in the gap between demo and production. I close that gap end to end — strategy, design, code, security, launch — by the same pair of hands rather than a relay race across vendors."**
   - Problem: "Most AI ideas die in the gap between demo and production" is a strong pain-led opener. But "I close that gap end to end" immediately pivots to describing the operator. The pain-led energy lasts one sentence, then flips to a credential. Also: "same pair of hands" already appears in the hero sub-line. One instance is memorable; two starts to feel like a tagline.
   - Rewrite: `Most AI ideas die in the gap between demo and production. Strategy, design, code, security, launch — one operator, one signature, no relay race.`

2. **"Your AI works in the notebook. Production is a different stack — eval infrastructure, orchestration, retrieval, deployment. I run that stack as the shipping discipline most AI founders skip."**
   - Problem: Strong opener ("Your AI works in the notebook" names the buyer's current state in seven words). The third sentence is good but "the shipping discipline most AI founders skip" makes a claim about the market that the buyer may or may not accept. It's a positioning assertion, not evidence.
   - Rewrite: `Your AI works in the notebook. Production is a different stack — eval infrastructure, orchestration, retrieval, deployment. I run that stack.` (Let the tier details below do the proving.)

3. **"Sounding board for model choice, eval design, failure-mode mitigation."** (AI Advisory tier, scope)
   - Problem: "Sounding board" survived from pre-Pass-23 copy. It's soft — it names a relationship posture, not an activity. The /services main page fixed this for GTM Advisory ("Monthly positioning review + narrative pressure-test against live deals") but the AI subpage still uses "sounding board."
   - Rewrite: `AI architecture review + monthly LLM strategy pressure-test. Model choice, eval design, failure-mode mitigation.`

4. **"Continuous integration of AI work + first iteration of eval framework + prompt-deployment pipeline live in month one."** (AI Retainer, first-month deliverable)
   - Problem: "Continuous integration of AI work" is vague. What artifact ships? The GTM retainer's deliverable names three specific things (positioning shift + 90-day roadmap + first sales enablement update). This one names a process ("continuous integration") rather than an artifact.
   - Rewrite: `First eval framework iteration shipped + prompt-deployment pipeline live + documented failure-mode inventory in month one.`

5. **"Full AI architecture + production deployment + eval infrastructure + team trained by end of month one."** (AI Embedded, first-month deliverable)
   - Problem: Four deliverables in one month is aggressive. If this is real, it's impressive. If a buyer reads it and thinks "no one ships all four of those in 30 days," the line hurts credibility. Consider: is month one really "full AI architecture + production deployment + eval infrastructure + team trained"? Or is month one "AI architecture documented + first production deployment + eval infrastructure foundations + team onboarded"?
   - Rewrite: `AI architecture documented + first production deployment shipped + eval infrastructure foundations live + team onboarded by end of month one.`

**Cuts:**
- The meta description still includes "No published day rates; engagement shapes are explicit." — the "no published day rates" clause is defensive. Cut it from the meta desc.

## Cross-cutting patterns

1. **Every case study repeats its dek as the opening paragraph.** Guardicore, Ordani, and HR-equity all have a summary paragraph immediately after the dek that says the same thing the dek just said. This is the single most consistent copy defect on the site. The fix is structural: cut all three opening summary paragraphs. The dek IS the summary. Let the reader land on "The problem" directly.

2. **"Sounding board" survived in one place.** The /services main page replaced it in the GTM Advisory tier (good — "narrative pressure-test against live deals" is much stronger). But the /services/ai-engineering subpage Advisory tier still uses "Sounding board for model choice, eval design, failure-mode mitigation." Fix the straggler.

3. **"Same pair of hands" appears twice.** Once in the hero sub-line, once in the /services End-to-end product description. A signature phrase loses its punch on the second use. Keep it in the hero; rephrase in the services desc.

4. **"Second brain on call" appears twice.** Once in the HR-equity case study Approach §04, once in the AI engineering subpage engagement-shapes intro. The HR-equity usage is native to that case study. The AI subpage usage is borrowed. Cut it from the AI subpage.

5. **Inconsistent practice/doula count language.** The home page says "fourteen practices." The /work index and Ordani case study say "14 doulas." These are different things. Align everything to "fourteen practices" or "14 practices."

6. **Claim-then-evidence vs. evidence-then-claim.** The strongest copy on the site (Guardicore outcome, HR-equity approach steps) lets the evidence speak first and trusts the reader to draw the conclusion. The weakest copy (Operating Principles #5 and #6, parts of the revenue dek) states the conclusion and then shows the evidence, which reads defensive. The pattern: if a sentence ends with "I know what X looks like," cut it. The preceding sentence already proved it.

7. **Two different nav structures.** The home page and /services pages use one nav (Clients, Ordani, Work, Contact). The /about, /work, and case study pages use a different nav (work, about, work with me, contact). The wordmark switches between "MICAH/JONES" and "MICAH JONES." This is a design issue, not a copy issue, but the inconsistency is visible and jarring when navigating between the two route groups.
