# Juror gate: did this evolution improve the page, or not?

You are given EIGHT screenshots of the same consulting home page, in two versions,
captured the same way: 1440 wide, viewport height, after fonts loaded and after every
entrance animation had settled, scrolled top to bottom in 900px steps. So each image is a
finished frame, and image N of one set is the same scroll depth as image N of the other.

  IMAGES 1-4 = BEFORE. The version LIVE today at www.micahjonesconsulting.com.
  IMAGES 5-8 = AFTER.  The same page after this pass. Not published.

What changed, so you judge the intent and not a guess at it:

  - A new section sits directly under the hero: "The Audit. $2,500. Two weeks." This is the
    first specific price the page has ever carried above the fold.
  - The receipts ledger MOVED UP, from far down the page into the slot right after the
    offer, so proof lands before any further pitch.
  - A section called "Three engagements" (Positioning & GTM / End-to-end product building /
    Frontier AI engineering) was CUT entirely.
  - "How I work" was INVERTED: the named deliverable is now the large type, the verb is a
    small label above it, and a named deal sits under each step as proof.
  - The hero gained one quiet line naming the buyer, and lost one of its three CTAs.

The buyer: a solo builder or small team who shipped something with Claude Code, Cursor,
Lovable, v0 or Bolt that demos well and then stalled before production. Technical enough to
smell fluff. Deciding whether this person is worth $2,500.

Answer these five and nothing else.

1. BETTER OR WORSE. Overall, is AFTER a better page than BEFORE for that buyer? Give a
   verdict, not a balanced summary. If it is worse, say so plainly and say where.

2. THE OFFER SECTION (image 6). This is the change I am least sure of. It is a big headline,
   a three-line paragraph, and a quiet right-aligned link, on a full terracotta field with a
   lot of empty space below. Does it read as a confident offer, or as a thin section with a
   headline and nothing under it? If it is thin, say specifically what belongs in that space.
   Note two constraints: the page allows only ONE filled button and the hero already uses it,
   and every section on this page carries the same 130px top and bottom padding, so the
   spacing is systemic and not specific to this section.

3. THE PROCESS SECTION. "How I work" now leads with the deliverable in large type, with the
   verb as a small label and a monospace proof line under it. Does inverting it make it sell
   better than a list of verbs did, or has it lost the scannability the verbs gave it?

4. MOVING THE RECEIPTS UP. Proof now arrives immediately after the price instead of far down
   the page. Does that ordering help or hurt? Consider that the buyer now meets a price
   before they have read any evidence.

5. THE THREE CHANGES you would make next, ranked, each concrete enough to hand to a builder.

SCOPE. Do not propose a client logo wall, testimonials, stock photography, illustration,
icon kits, a second accent colour, or a monospace change. Those are settled. Do not propose
replacing the rotating-word headline; that is operator-locked and has been reversed twice.
Judge the composition, hierarchy, typography and order of what is in front of you.

Be decisive and specific. No preamble. Answer 1 through 5 under those headings.
