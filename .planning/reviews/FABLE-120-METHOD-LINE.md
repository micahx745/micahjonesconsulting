# FABLE-120: the method line on /work

Fable judge look, 2026-09-16. Read-only. One sentence, set large on paper under the lead study,
above five studies and the record rows. Operator: "reword with help from fable"; he likes the
theme (it matches the Reddit research and both audiences he named) and keeps the audiences

Checked against: ruling 1 in FABLE-120-RECORD.md; the Voice section; `brand.json.voice.banned`;
DESIGN_BAR R7 (one assertion, ~2 seconds) and R16 (only this operator); the ledger rows of
2026-09-15 and 2026-09-16 (RFP client not repositioned, its value is reach beyond the network;
290K and 36× dead; $20M is "revenue behind my work"; birth worker one to three to five to ten,

## Six candidates

1. **"I find what your buyers are actually paying for, then build the system that sells it."**
   (16 words)
   Reason: the ruling's two halves with the meta clause cut; the control the operator already saw.
   Risk: "it" can be read as the system, and it is the candidate a stranger could most easily
   put on another consultant's card; the studies below do the R16 work for it.

2. **"I find what your buyers are actually paying for, then build the system that sells exactly
   that."** (17 words)
   Reason: closes the loop the mock leaves open and states the Reddit insight in the line
   itself: sell the thing they are paying for, not the thing you planned.
   Risk: two adverbs in one sentence ("actually", "exactly"); a craft pass may call it fussy.

3. **"I find what your buyers are actually paying for, then build the thing that sells it."**
   (16 words)
   Reason: "thing" is how he talks; it covers a story, a booking path and an engine without
   sounding like software only.
   Risk: "thing" at hero scale can read as unwritten, and the SMB audience hears "system" as
   the word for what they are buying.

4. **"Before I build anything for you, I find what your buyers are actually paying for."**
   (15 words)
   Reason: names the discipline that separates him from every builder both audiences have
   already hired: find first, build second.
   Risk: it drops the sell half, so the $14M and $3M above and below it have no verb in the
   line; "for you" is a shade salesy.

5. **"I find what your buyers are actually paying for, then build the story and the software
   that sell it."** (19 words)
   Reason: the line itself carries R16: the record shows him building both, and only he has both.
   Risk: it re-splits him into two trades, the exact failure ruling 1 named ("three consultants
   sharing a surname"); "story" is agency vocabulary; two nouns slow the two-second read.

6. **"I find what your buyers are actually paying for, then build the system that sells it
   without you in the room."** (21 words)
   Reason: the tail is the sentence the owner who hires assistants wants to read, and it is true
   of the RFP engine, the content engine and the booking path.
   Risk: 21 words, past the cap; at Guardicore he was the man in the room, and that study sits
   directly above the line; the tail reads as a promise.

## Recommendation

Candidate 2. It keeps the theme he liked word for word, resolves the one ambiguity in the mock,
and puts the point of the method in the sentence rather than leaving it to the studies: sell
what they are paying for. It is true above all five studies, including the RFP engine, where the
finding was done by the software scoring bids against the expert's own work and not by a
repositioning the ledger forbids. Fallback if "exactly" is judged fussy: candidate 1 as written.

Truth check, candidate 2, one line per study:
- Guardicore: banks were signing for east-west visibility; he moved the story to that and sold it.
- RFP engine: the engine finds the bids the author's own work wins and drafts them; buyers beyond
  the network, no repositioning claimed.
- Ordani: practitioners were paying six tools for one platform nobody had built; he built the one.
- Content engine: rooms were paying for the message; the engine turned one video into the week
  that carries it, one stream into four.
- Birth worker: buyers were paying for one of the things she did; he rebuilt what a stranger reads
  first so they buy the range.

## The proposed version's weakness

"...and the record shows it in money" makes the page talk about itself, pushes the line to 24
words and past two seconds, and edges toward the result promise the line was told not to make;
the record below already shows the money, so the clause only repeats it.
