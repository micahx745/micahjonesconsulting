# Paste this into the consulting-site session

---

I need you to produce a package called **`02-BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md`**. It will be
combined with a Reddit-evidence package from another session and handed to a
research model that will recommend changes to my wording, services, playbook,
site design and SEO.

**Write it to a file. Do not summarise for me in chat — produce the document.**

## The single most important rule

**Quote verbatim, do not paraphrase.** The research chat's whole job is to
compare *my exact sentences* against *the market's exact sentences*. A summary
of my positioning is useless to it; the literal H1 is the unit of analysis. If a
page says "The gap is positioning, not features", write that, not "we emphasise
positioning".

Where you are inferring rather than reading, say so inline with `[INFERRED]`.
Where a fact has a source and a date, give both. Where you don't know, write
`UNKNOWN` — a gap is usable, a guess is not.

## What the other package already contains, so don't duplicate it

A year of Reddit measured bottom-up: 5,456 posts, ten subreddits, what people
say they're stuck on, and which rooms the pain and the money live in. It has
market language. It has **nothing** about me, my site, my offers or my product.
That is your entire job.

## Produce these nine sections, in this order

### 1. Identity and constraints
Who I am in one paragraph, as the site presents me. Solo or team. Time available
per week for delivery. Any hard constraints — geography, industries I won't take,
NDAs, a day job, capacity ceilings. What I will not do at any price.

### 2. The offers, exactly as sold
For every offer: **verbatim name**, verbatim price, verbatim one-line
description, what is actually delivered, typical duration, and where it appears
on the site. Include the playbook and any free or lead-magnet offer. Flag which
have ever actually sold, and how many times, if that is knowable — **and mark it
`UNKNOWN` if it is not, rather than estimating.**

### 3. Verbatim copy inventory
This is the section the research chat will lean on hardest.
- Every **H1** on every page, quoted exactly, with its URL path.
- Every **title tag** and **meta description**, quoted exactly.
- The **hero paragraph** of the home page, services page and playbook page, quoted in full.
- Every **CTA button label**, quoted exactly.
- The three or four sentences anywhere on the site that state the customer's *problem* — these are the ones that will be tested against market language.

### 4. The playbook product
Full table of contents. Chapter titles verbatim. Page count. Price and any price
history. The list of companion files with their filenames. The book's central
claim in its own words. Who it says it is for, verbatim. What it promises the
reader will be able to do afterwards. Any known reader feedback, positive or
negative, quoted.

### 5. Claims and their provenance
Every factual claim the site or the book makes that a stranger could challenge —
numbers, company names, outcomes, credentials, customer descriptors. For each:
the claim verbatim, its source, its date, and whether it is currently defensible.
If there is a claims ledger or lessons-learned file, reproduce its operative
rules rather than describing them. **Include the standing prohibitions** — things
I have already ruled must never be said, and why.

### 6. Evidence I already hold
Real client outcomes, case studies, testimonials, before/after metrics —
verbatim, with permission status noted. Anything I can legitimately cite. Also:
what evidence I conspicuously lack.

### 7. Traffic, funnel and current SEO reality
Whatever is actually known: traffic volume, sources, top landing pages, current
keyword rankings, conversion events, email list size, where enquiries come from
today. Technical SEO state — sitemap, schema, page speed, indexation. **Mark
every unknown as `UNKNOWN`.** A funnel with three real numbers and six unknowns
is more useful than nine estimates.

### 8. Design and brand constraints
The design system: typefaces, palette with hex values, spacing rules, the
motion policy. Any brand rules that constrain copy — banned words, sentence
length caps, voice rules, first-person conventions. The stack the site is built
on and what a change actually costs to ship. Anything already ruled out
aesthetically.

### 9. What I have already tried, and what I already believe
Positioning I have tested and abandoned, and why. Channels tried and dropped.
Copy I changed and what happened. And — separately and honestly labelled — the
things I currently *believe* about my market that are assumptions rather than
findings. **This section protects the research chat from re-recommending
something I already know failed, and flags my priors so it can challenge them
rather than confirm them.**

## Format

Markdown. Headings exactly as numbered above. Verbatim quotes in blockquotes or
inline quotes so they are unambiguously distinguishable from your description of
them. Tables where the data is tabular. No preamble, no closing summary.

## One thing to resist

Do not make the business sound good. The research chat is going to be asked to
recommend changes, and it can only do that against an honest picture. If a page
is weak, an offer has never sold, or a claim is thin, say so plainly in the
document. The failure mode here is a flattering brief that produces flattering
advice.
