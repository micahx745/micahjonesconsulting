## Deliverable 2 — Diagnosis of the live site

I read the live homepage in full. The internal pages would not open for me directly, so this diagnosis is anchored on the homepage copy I could verify plus the sentences you quoted. The automated checks are clean, which confirms your own read: the problem is not banned words. It is structure and rhythm.

### The ten worst sentences, with the defect

1. **"I take AI-built products from demo to production."** (Homepage H1.) Not wrong, but it is a category claim with no tension. It tells me what you do, not why I am stuck. Compare your own better line buried further down. The hero is the weakest-working sentence on the page.
2. **"*I also position products and build the go-to-market that sells them.* Strategy and software, shipped by the same pair of hands."** (Homepage subhead.) "The same pair of hands" is a stock phrase. "Strategy and software" is abstraction stacked on abstraction. Two clauses, zero concrete image.
3. **"The demo took a weekend. The last 20% is eating your month."** (Homepage, packages teaser.) This is actually a strong sentence. The defect is placement and, per your corpus, aim: it targets the regression loop, which is ~9x rarer than "it shipped, nobody came." Good writing, wrong problem. Move the good rhythm to the right pain.
4. **"Operating principles / How I work."** (Homepage section head.) Template heading. "Operating principles" is the single most consultant-template phrase on the page. It signals deck, not person.
5. **"1. 01 Diagnose … 2. 02 Build … 3. 03 Position."** (Homepage principle grid.) The numbered 01/02/03 grid is the core of the machine feel. It reads as a framework generated to fill three slots. Real operators do not think in evenly-weighted triads.
6. **"Every engagement ships a named artifact in month one. No decks. No discovery debt."** (Homepage, step 02.) "Discovery debt" is a coined-jargon wall. "Named artifact" is abstraction where a concrete noun should be. You built an RFP engine and a HIPAA CRM; name those, do not say "named artifact."
7. **"The receipts."** (Homepage, metrics section head.) Borrowed internet slang used as a section header. It reads as reaching for tone. The numbers under it are strong enough to not need a slang label.
8. **"Built for the people who show up for mothers."** (Homepage, Ordani.) This one nearly works and is the warmest line on the site. The defect is it sits under an image stack and a paragraph that immediately turns technical ("HIPAA is the law"), so the warmth does not carry.
9. **"Too big for duct tape. Not ready for an agency retainer. You get me directly."** (Homepage, engagements card.) "Too big for duct tape" is a cute abstraction that makes the reader decode. The three-fragment rhythm is the exact "punchy fragments in threes" AI tell the a16z piece names.
10. **"NAME THE PROBLEM →"** (Homepage, closing CTA.) Shouted, vague, and it asks the reader to do the work of framing. A CTA should carry the reader's intent, not assign homework. Compare Lily Zheng's CTA that names the reader's confusion for them.

Your homepage lists **SurveyMonkey at "$2.33B — IPO, first-day value."** Your verified list gives SurveyMonkey as a Nasdaq IPO (2018) with your own contribution as "$1M+ toward the IPO" and does not authorize a $2.33B first-day figure. ### Your structural hypothesis: confirmed, and sharpened

You are right that the machine feel is rhythm and sameness, not vocabulary. The specific culprits, in priority order:

- **The evenly-weighted triad.** 01/02/03 principles, three packages, three things-to-do, three-fragment sentences. Everything comes in threes of equal weight. Humans emphasize one thing and subordinate the rest. The relentless parallelism is the tell.
- **The preview-card formula.** Every case study is compressed into the same "Name / Role / Bold metric →" card. Repeated five times, it stops reading as evidence and starts reading as a template loop.
- **Template headings.** "Operating principles," "Every engagement includes," "The receipts." These are slots, not sentences a person would say out loud.
- **Scoreboard framing.** "$20M+ / Four exits / $5B+" stacked as a metrics wall. The numbers are genuinely strong, but presented as a scoreboard they read as a pitch deck slide, not as a claim a person is making to you.

### Sections that should not exist as they are

- **The 01/02/03 "How I work" grid.** Delete the numbering and the triad. Replace with two or three unequal paragraphs where one clearly matters most.
- **"The receipts" scoreboard as a standalone unit.** Fold the numbers into prose where they support a claim. A number in a sentence persuades; a number on a scoreboard advertises.
- **The stacked Ordani image gallery on the sales homepage.** You have a standing decision against stock imagery; these are real, but four captioned images mid-homepage break the read and push the buyer's next action below the fold.

### The real complaint: does the page make anyone want to work with *you*?

No, and this is the core failure. The page lists what you did. It does not show how you think. Every strong solo site I read (Critchlow, Stark, patio11) sells the mind, not the résumé. Your Guardicore story is the most interesting thing you have — you found that buyers could not see traffic between their own systems, moved the whole pitch, and sat in the deals — and on the homepage it is compressed to "message/buyer mismatch found · $14M." The one sentence that would make someone want to hire you is the one you cut. That is the pattern across the page: the template ate the person. ## Deliverable 3 — The audience ruling

### The question

Your five clients run from a solo birth worker to a Tel Aviv enterprise. Your brand file names two audiences at once: $5–50M founders, and Black HR consultants, doulas, birth workers, equity practitioners. Add the hiring manager. That is three fundamentally different readers. You offered four options: lead with one buyer, lead with the shared problem, two doors near the top, or address everyone.

### The ruling: lead with the shared problem. One homepage. A separate, lightly-linked page for the hiring manager.

**Why the shared problem wins.** Your corpus finding is the decider: the pain and the money are in different rooms, but the *pain* is the same shape everywhere. In business subreddits people are stuck on distribution; in developer subreddits people hit an engineering wall. Both are the gap between "I built it" and "it works in the market." A doula who cannot get bookings, a founder whose demo will not reach production, and a security company whose pitch is aimed at the wrong buyer are the same story: the build is not the bottleneck. One sentence can hold all three if it names that gap and not a persona.

**Why not lead with one buyer.** Leading with the founder loses the birth worker in the first ten seconds, and your standing test is that she feels welcomed immediately. Leading with the birth worker miscasts you for the enterprise buyer who pays your largest fees. April Dunford's own rule cuts against single-persona leads when your differentiated value is genuinely cross-segment: you position on the value a well-defined segment cares about, and here the segment is defined by a *situation* (stuck between building and selling), not a demographic.

**Why not two doors at the top.** Two doors force the visitor to self-classify before they trust you. It doubles the cognitive load in the exact ten seconds you need to earn. It also visually re-creates the triad problem. Doors belong one scroll down, as "here's where you probably are," not as the hero.

**Why not address everyone.** That is what the site does now, and it is why it reads generic. Addressing everyone is addressing no one.

### The mechanism

- **Hero:** one situation sentence everyone recognizes, plus one line on who you are, in the first person, with one named number.
- **One scroll down:** two or three unequal "you are probably here" paths (the founder, the solo practitioner, the team that needs an operator). These route to packages or to an engagement email. This is where segmentation lives, after trust, not before it.
- **Ordani** stays on the homepage but as proof of range and mission, written in the Website Doula register, not the technical register.
- **The hiring manager** gets a dedicated page. Call it **"Work with me full-time"** or **"For hiring teams."** Link it in the footer and once from the About page. Do not link it in the primary nav. On that page, follow Critchlow: lead with how you think and what you have owned (thirteen years, four exits worked inside, $20M+ behind your work), not with package prices. A hiring manager who lands on a page selling $500 sessions bounces; a hiring manager who lands on a page about judgment and range converts.

### Is your ranking of the three actions wrong?

Half wrong. Packages first, self-serve, no call: correct, and it matches both the productized-services literature and your own revenue logic. But you have the engagement email and the corporate role sitting at similar weight in your head, and on the page they should not be. Ranking:

1. **Buy a package (self-serve).** Homepage hero and primary CTA.
2. **Email about an engagement.** Homepage, one scroll down, after the paths.
3. **Corporate role.** Footer link to a dedicated page. Real, but not a homepage job.

The corporate role is the reader most damaged by proximity to a $500 button, so it moves furthest away. This is not a demotion of its importance to you; it is protection of its conversion.

### What it costs you

- You lose the immediate "I do X for people like you" clarity that a single-persona site gives. The shared-problem lead is slightly more abstract by nature, so the situation sentence has to be concrete or the whole thing collapses into the generic you are escaping.
- The birth-worker and equity-practitioner audiences will feel *welcomed* but not *targeted*. If a real slice of your revenue is meant to come directly from them buying services (not via Ordani), a shared-problem homepage under-serves them and you would need a dedicated services-for-practitioners page too.
- Splitting the hiring-manager reader onto its own page means some hiring managers never find it. That is the correct trade: the ones who dig are the ones worth talking to.

### What would make this ruling wrong

- If your actual money is coming mostly from one segment (say, 70%+ from founders), lead with that segment and treat the rest as secondary. The shared-problem lead is correct only if your revenue really is spread across the range, as your five cases suggest.
- If the corporate role is your real near-term goal and consulting is the side, invert the whole thing: the site becomes a portfolio with a "currently open to full-time roles" banner, and packages move down.
- If analytics show the homepage bounce is worst among first-time practitioner visitors, the shared-problem sentence is too abstract for them and you need the two-path split higher up.

## Deliverable 5 — The blog and SEO plan

### What actually works for ranking in 2026 for a one-person site with no domain authority

The landscape shifted hard, and it shifted in your favor. The verified reality:

- **Experience is now the primary ranking differentiator.** Google's March 2026 core update (rolled out March 27–April 8, 2026) made first-hand experience the thing that separates winners from losers on contested queries. As analysts summarized it, "content demonstrating genuine first-hand experience outperforms comprehensive but impersonal information… the Experience component of E-E-A-T… is now the primary differentiator on contested keywords." This is your entire advantage. You have done the things most writers only research.
- **Rankings no longer equal citations, and that helps small sites.** Per Ahrefs' February 2026 analysis of 863,000 keyword SERPs and roughly 4 million AI Overview URLs, only 38% of URLs cited in AI Overviews also appeared in the organic top 10, down from 76% in its July 2025 study. A well-structured answer on a low-authority domain can be pulled into an AI answer that a decade of link-building could not buy.
- **Reddit is a top-tier citation channel and you already have the corpus.** Per Semrush's three-month, 230,000-prompt study, "Reddit and LinkedIn were among the top five most-cited domains on ChatGPT, Google's AI Mode, and Perplexity"; Stacc's data puts Reddit at 46.7% of Perplexity citations and 21% of Google AI Overview citations. But it is volatile: per Semrush, "ChatGPT cited Reddit in close to 60% of prompt responses in early August before collapsing to around 10% by mid-September" 2025, tied to Google removing the num=100 parameter around September 11, 2025. Treat Reddit as one channel, not the plan.
- **What is slow or not worth it:** Domain authority via link-building is slow; do not chase it. The llms.txt tactic has been falsified: per Ahrefs' study published June 15, 2026 (authored by Louise Linehan and Xibeijia Guan), of about 38,000 valid llms.txt files across 137,210 domains, "97% received zero requests in May 2026. Not few requests. None." Do not spend an hour on it. Programmatic/mass-generated pages are actively penalized under the helpful-content system; for you they are both risky and off-brand.
- **What to actually do:** Answer-first blocks. Put a direct 2–3 sentence answer at the top of each section, because per Kevin Indig's analysis of 3 million ChatGPT responses and 18,012 verified citations, it is "statistically indisputable: 44.2% of citations come from the first 30% of content" (his "ski ramp" pattern). Use question-shaped H2s that match what people type. Write self-contained sections that survive being lifted out of context. Build a real author entity: one consistent bio, same name, linked from every post. Internal-link from posts to the relevant case study and package. Add basic Article/FAQ schema. Realistic timeline: 3–6 months before compounding, longer for anything competitive.
