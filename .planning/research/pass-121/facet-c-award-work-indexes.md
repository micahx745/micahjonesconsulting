# Pass-121 / Facet C — Award-level work indexes and case studies, 2025-2026

Scope: Awwwards, FWA, CSS Design Awards, Godly, Siteinspire, Land-book and the live sites of
studios/independents. All findings below are from live WebSearch/WebFetch this session
(2026-09-16) unless flagged otherwise. Pass-120's 14 references (COLLINS Mailchimp, Instrument
PagerDuty, Koto Amazon, Wolff Olins Instacart, Basement Studio Harvey, Clay Sky, Studio Freight
Brex, NYT Snow Fall, The Pudding film dialogue, Works in Progress housing, April Dunford, Dan
Mall Spine, Harpal Singh, UXGen Advisory) are **not re-proposed** as new candidates but are cited
where directly relevant. Basement Studio's Harvey write-up is examined again here from a
different, narrower angle (the case study's own no-photography visual devices) since Pass-120
studied it for other reasons.

## Central claims

**C1.** Locomotive (Montreal) has been named Awwwards Agency of the Year for seven consecutive
years, most recently announced February 2025. Its `/work` index (`locomotive.ca/en/work`) is a
tag-filterable grid ("All projects," Branding, Digital, Experience, E-commerce, Content, each
with a live count) sorted newest-first, with award badges inline on the card (e.g. "©2025 4
awards" on Lightship). URL: https://locomotive.ca/en/work. Date: agency-of-the-year announcement
Feb 2025; index content live as fetched 2026-09-16. Evidence: live-example (fetched) +
vendor-claim (the "Agency of the Year" framing is Awwwards/Locomotive's own PR).
Means for this site: a persistent, visible award/count badge on a card is a cheap, honest way to
carry credibility on an index entry without a photo — this site could badge case studies with a
one-line outcome number instead (it already does something like this in a "record block").

**C2.** Bakken & Bæck's `/work` page (bakkenbaeck.com/work) lists roughly two dozen projects
(Sierra, Legora, Google DeepMind, IKEA Voice, Ramp, World Labs, Phantom, CoinTracker, etc.) as a
vertical grid where each card carries 2-4 stacked thumbnail previews plus service tags (Brand /
Web / Product) rather than one hero image per entry, framed explicitly as "an overview of recent
case studies, as well as a selection of our self-initiated projects." URL:
https://bakkenbaeck.com/work. Date: live as fetched 2026-09-16. Evidence: live-example.
Means for this site: a multi-thumbnail card (2-3 stacked crops) reads as thicker evidence than
one photo per case study — useful where the site has "one real photograph" per study; stacking a
photo with a screenshot/diagram/quote card in the same tile could stretch thin material.

**C3.** Unseen Studio (unseen.co), profiled by Codrops 2026-07-20, is an independent
brand/digital/motion studio whose case studies (CLOU, Cosmos, Symphony of Vines) are written as
narrative sections rather than a thumbnail index: each opens with a large signature visual (a
"bespoke 3D project ring" for CLOU, not a photograph) and states the brief-to-solution arc in
prose alongside the motion, explicitly framing animation as communicating "the platform's vision"
rather than decorating it — the same device the studio uses for Symphony of Vines, built from
"abstract visuals, motion and sound" rather than any photography. URL:
https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/20/the-craft-behind-memorable-digital-experiences-inside-unseen-studio/
(profile); unseen.co (studio site, not independently re-verified by direct fetch this session —
see Live examples table). Date: 2026-07-20. Evidence: primary-doc (studio's own account, relayed
via Codrops interview) — treat the studio's framing as vendor-claim, the technique names as
primary-doc.
Means for this site: "motion that states the point of the case study" (not just an entrance) is
the sharper standard than "one signature motion" as decoration — worth naming explicitly in a
brief for the ORDANI or RFP study hero; and a custom abstract-graphic device (ring, plane, etc.)
is a proven substitute for a photograph an NDA won't allow.

**C4.** Cuberto (cuberto.com) holds an Awwwards "Agency of the Year" credit and multiple 2026
Honorable Mentions (Sweeping Corp, May 2026; FIND Real Estate, March 2026; Born & Bred, February
2026). Evidence: vendor-claim (Cuberto's own "Agency of the Year" framing, echoed by third-party
design blogs) + live-example (Awwwards listing pages). URL: https://www.awwwards.com/cuberto/.
Date: 2026 (multiple dates above). Means for this site: nothing directly actionable beyond
confirming that repeated Honorable Mentions (not just one Site of the Day) are how a studio index
reads as sustained craft rather than a lucky hit — relevant framing for how Micah presents his own
"record block."

**C5.** Iventions (iventions.com), an events/experience studio, won CSS Design Awards "Website of
the Month" (October 2025), was a Website of the Year 2025 finalist, and took an Awwwards Site of
the Day and Developer Award. Verified live 2026-09-16: hero is "Step into the Spotlight" with a
muted showreel toggle and a project carousel; a third-party review (Utsubo) describes "a Three.js
scene [that] treats each project like a spotlit installation, with GSAP pacing the reveals so the
page reads like a guided walk-through rather than a grid" — this session's own WebFetch of the
live page did not itself surface Three.js in the rendered markup (expected, since it is
client-rendered), so the Three.js claim is corroborated by a third party, not independently
confirmed by this session. URLs: https://iventions.com/ (verified live) and
https://www.cssdesignawards.com/wotm/iventions/48253/ (award record). Date: October 2025 (WOTM).
Evidence: live-example (homepage fetch) + expert-opinion (Utsubo's technique description).
Means for this site: "spotlit installation" pacing — one project fully lit and framed at a time,
paced by scroll-triggered motion rather than shown in a grid, and driven largely by video/motion
rather than stills — is a concrete pattern for a five-study `/work` index that has almost no
photography.

**C6.** Pentagram's Reddit identity case study (pentagram.com/work/reddit, underlying project
dated 2022-2023) carries its story with almost no photography: embedded video (Snoo's 2D-to-3D
transformation, described as "comparable to Pixar animation"), two custom typefaces (Reddit Sans,
Reddit Display) used as the primary graphic device, and a recurring conversation-bubble motif
engineered as "a dynamic content device" that frames both text and imagery throughout. Structure:
context, four brand-positioning traits, visual-identity evolution, named design decisions
(character, type, motif, motion, color), credits. URL: https://www.pentagram.com/work/reddit.
Date: underlying project 2022-2023 (pre-2025, background only), page live as fetched 2026-09-16.
Evidence: live-example (page fetch) — cited for the still-current *pattern* (type plus motif plus
video in place of photos), not as a 2025-2026 event.
Means for this site: a single recurring graphic motif (like this site's hand-drawn underline)
used as a literal content device — framing pull quotes, section breaks, and data points, not just
decorating headlines — is a concrete way to carry a wordy case study with one motif doing
structural work.

Evidence: primary-doc (studio's own post) + expert-opinion (Codrops profile).
Means for this site: directly the closest precedent to rfp-engine and content-engine — an NDA'd
AI/B2B client, told through typography and motion with no product screenshots.

**C8.** By-Kin (by-kin.com), a UK commercial-interiors/branding/graphic-design studio, won
Awwwards Site of the Day, the Awwwards Developer Award, an FWA, and CSS Design Awards Web of the
Day for its own site (Next.js, GSAP, Strapi). Verified live 2026-09-16: the homepage is in fact
**photography-driven** — a rotating image carousel of interior/branding work is the hero, with
grid case-study tiles (Dentons, 5 St Paul's, JD Sports Canteen) — correcting an initial
third-party summary that described it as purely typographic/motion. The Developer Award
specifically cites "the engineering... motion system, performance, and code quality" — GSAP,
ScrollTrigger, and Lenis smooth-scroll are named as the stack by a third-party technical write-up.
URLs: https://by-kin.com/ (verified live, portrait-mode notice present) and
https://www.hontran.dev/blog/by-kin-case-study-award-winning-website (technical write-up). Date:
2026 (award month unconfirmed from available sources). Evidence: live-example (homepage fetch,
corrects an earlier characterization) + expert-opinion (hontran.dev technical breakdown).
Means for this site: a caution, not a technique to copy directly — proof that a Developer Award
can be won by restrained motion craft (GSAP plus Lenis, no gimmicks) even when the studio does
have real photography, which this site mostly does not; the craft, not the photo count, is what
gets judged.

**C9.** Giulio Collesei's portfolio ("They Call Me Giulio," theycallmegiulio.com), profiled by
Codrops 2026-04-14, is a solo creative technologist's four-scene cinematic portfolio (Blade
Runner-, Dragon Ball-, Matrix-, and noir-referencing scenes) built in pure Three.js with WebGPU
and TSL shaders, React Router, GSAP, Lenis, and Blender-authored 3D assets, using KTX2/AVIF
compression to keep it performant. URL:
https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/14/they-call-me-giulio-the-making-of-a-cinematic-cyberpunk-portfolio/.
Date: 2026-04-14. Evidence: primary-doc (maker's own technical account via Codrops).
Means for this site: an extreme example of "solo operator, no template" — useful as an outer
bound of what's possible, and a caution: this level of bespoke WebGPU/shader work is not
appropriate for a wordy consulting site and would read as showy against the owner's own stated
goal of not looking machine-built.

**C10.** Joseph Santamaria's portfolio (joseph-san.com), profiled by Codrops 2026-04-28, replaces
a navbar with a scroll-driven 3D environment (Three.js/WebGL, GSAP ScrollTrigger/Observer/
SplitText, Blender, Draco/KTX2 compression) and opens projects with a "tesseract unfolding"
transition, sustaining 144fps via GPU instancing and dynamic culling. He self-describes as a
"Creative Software Engineer & UI/UX designer" — an independent engineer selling technical craft,
not only visual design. URL:
https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/28/more-than-a-portfolio-building-a-scroll-driven-3d-world-with-something-to-say/.
Date: 2026-04-28. Evidence: primary-doc.
Means for this site: same caution as C9 — a real independent-engineer precedent, but pitched far
past this site's own rules against pinning/parallax/cursor-following; useful only as the far edge
of the spectrum against which to calibrate how modest this site's ask ("stand out a little")
actually is.

Means for this site: closest class-3 precedent to Micah's actual positioning (a solo operator

client-logo band plus testimonial plus stacked-image work tiles as *proof* substituting for full

**C12.** Tom Critchlow (tomcritchlow.com), an independent strategy consultant (author of "The
Strategic Independent," current work at Alephic on AI and marketing), runs a deliberately
minimal, text-only, whitespace-heavy site with no imagery, no client-logo wall, and no
testimonials — the blog and long-form writing are the credibility mechanism, not visual design.
URL: https://tomcritchlow.com/. Date: live as fetched 2026-09-16. Evidence: live-example.
Means for this site: a documented counter-case — proof that a real, successful independent
consultant can win high-value clients (cites NYT, Google, Angi, Finder) with almost zero visual
craft, because the writing carries it. Relevant tension for this site: Micah's ask is explicitly
for *more* visual craft, so this is evidence for the floor (content-led minimalism works) rather
than the ceiling — worth naming in the brief as the default the harness should not fall back to
just because it is safe.

## Now overused

- **Horizontal-scroll, filterable case-study grids** are flagged by multiple 2025-2026 round-ups
  (htmlburger, wixfresh) as a saturated pattern specifically because so many studio sites shipped
  one in the same season — evidence type: expert-opinion, no single measured study found this
  session.
- **The "portal/tesseract transition into a project"** (Joseph Santamaria, and echoed across
  other Codrops-profiled solo portfolios from the same 2026 run) is becoming a recognizable genre
  move among creative-technologist portfolios — evidence type: expert-opinion (pattern observed
  across multiple 2026 Codrops pieces read this session).
- **Client-logo walls** appear on nearly every solo consultant and agency site surveyed here
  (Anton Sten, general "marketing consultant" round-ups) — directly in tension with this site's
  own written rule against a client-logo wall; worth flagging as a rule this site should keep
  breaking from, not adopt, given how commodity the pattern now reads.
- **"Agency of the Year" and "Site of the Day" badges displayed on the awarded studio's own
  site** (Locomotive, Cuberto) are themselves becoming a genre convention — a credibility signal
  that now reads as expected rather than distinguishing, given how many studios surveyed here do
  it.

## Costs and risks

- **Performance.** Three.js and WebGPU-shader portfolios (C9, C10) and Three.js-installation
  studio sites (C5) carry real payload and GPU cost; none of the sources this session gave
  Lighthouse numbers, so any performance claim about these specific sites would be
  vendor-claim/unmeasured — flag as unverified if cited in a brief. This site's own
  Definition-of-Done requires Lighthouse Performance 95 or higher on mobile, which directly rules
  out anything resembling C9 or C10's approach.
- **Accessibility.** None of the fetched pages were checked for axe or contrast in this pass (out
  of scope for this facet); a reduced-motion fallback is mentioned nowhere in the sources found —
  this site's own `prefers-reduced-motion` rule (Definition-of-Done item 6) is stricter than
  anything observed here and should not be relaxed to chase these references.
- **Honesty and "looks machine-built" risk.** The owner's explicit ask is to not look
  machine-built. The WebGPU-shader, portal-transition genre (C9, C10) is presently a strong
  visual signal, in 2026 web-design discourse, of showing off what an assistant can build —
  adopting it wholesale would work against, not for, that stated goal. This is a reasoned
  inference from the pattern density observed, not a cited study.
- **Which written rule each item would relax, named explicitly.**
  - The Pentagram, Basement Studio, and Resn no-photography devices (C6, C7, C14) relax nothing
    in this site's rules — they are compatible with "no stock photography, illustration, icon
    kits, or 3D" because they use type, motion, and real 3D of owned assets rather than stock or
    decorative 3D.
  - Iventions' spotlit-installation pattern (C5) and Resn's interactive-brand-guidelines idea
    (C14) would require this site to reconsider "exactly one signature motion" — both use motion
    as a structural device across multiple moments, not once. Any adoption is a rule change, not
    an execution detail, and belongs to the operator's ruling, not a brief.
  - The client-logo-wall convention (C11 and general 2026 consultant-site round-ups) is directly
    this site's own banned pattern — the evidence here supports keeping the ban, not following
    the convention.

## What this site could take

1. **`/work` header and featured-study entry.** Adopt Locomotive's inline award/outcome badge
   (C1) on the "record block" already planned for `/work`: a one-line stat badge per case study
   (for example, "$14M, acquired by Akamai"), rendered the way Locomotive renders its award count,
   using the existing `content/citations.ts` numbers rather than new copy.
2. **Index entries.** Adopt Bakken & Bæck's multi-thumbnail card pattern (C2) adapted to NDA
   constraints: where guardicore or ordani have one real photograph, pair it in the same tile with
   a second, non-photographic panel (a typographic pull-stat or a motif graphic) so no card is a
   lonely single photo sitting next to four photo-less cards.
3. **Study hero.** Borrow Pentagram's recurring-motif-as-content-device pattern (C6): make this
   site's existing hand-drawn underline or hand-circle do literal structural work inside a case
   study (framing the outcome stat, marking section breaks) rather than only decorating headers,
   the way Reddit's conversation-bubble motif frames both text and images throughout that case
   study.
4. **Study body exhibits.** For the three fully NDA'd studies (rfp-engine, content-engine,
   birth-worker) with no imagery permitted at all, take the Basement Studio/Harvey and Resn
   Squarespace approach directly: build the exhibit from typography, a motion sequence, and where
   truthful, an anonymized system diagram, rather than defaulting to a stock-photo-adjacent
   placeholder.
5. **Motion vocabulary.** If the operator is willing to relax "exactly one signature motion" (a
   rule change, flagged above, not a brief decision), Iventions' scroll-paced "guided walkthrough"
   (C5) is the most transferable model: one motion grammar reused at every "spotlight" moment in a
   study, rather than one motion used exactly once on the whole site.
6. **AI discoverability plumbing.** None of the sources fetched this session addressed
   AI-crawler or LLM-discoverability directly (this facet's brief was award-craft, not
   SEO/AI plumbing); flagging as a gap for whichever other Pass-121 facet covers
   llms.txt, structured data, or AI-crawler concerns — this facet found no evidence either way and
   should not be treated as having cleared that question.
7. **Class-3 minimum-viable-upgrade fallback.** If a full five-study visual overhaul is too large
   for one pass, Buzz Usborne's six-tile "Recent Work" homepage (C13) and Anton Sten's
   stacked-image plus logo-band plus testimonial pattern (C11) are lighter-weight precedents than
   a full Awwwards-style rebuild, and both are real solo or small-consultancy sites, not agency
   fantasies — useful as the floor of "doesn't read as plain" rather than the ceiling.
8. **Restraint counter-example to keep in the brief.** Cite Tom Critchlow (C12) explicitly as the
   argument against over-rotating on visual spectacle: a real, high-value independent consultant
   site can and does win serious clients with near-zero visual craft. Naming this in the brief
   protects against the harness overshooting the owner's actual ask, which is to stand out a
   little, not to become an agency showcase.
