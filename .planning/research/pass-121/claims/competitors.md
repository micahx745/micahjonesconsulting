## Flag before using this file

No company literally named **"Luna"** that processes Medicaid or private-insurance claims for
doulas or birth workers turned up in live search. The company that matches the brief's description
almost word for word — free to join, handles credentialing/contracting, eligibility checks and
revenue-cycle management for a doula network billing Medicaid/insurance — is **Loula**
(joinloula.com), not Luna. "Loula" and "Luna" are phonetically close, and this looks like a
name mix-up somewhere upstream of this brief rather than two different companies. This needs an
operator ruling before the case study names anything: confirm whether "Loula" is the intended
competitor, or supply the actual "Luna" entity if one exists that this search missed. Do not let
the page ship naming "Luna" on the strength of this file alone.

## Luna, in detail

No operating company named "Luna" was found that files Medicaid or private-insurance claims for
doulas, midwives, or perinatal counselors. Search turned up many unrelated "Luna"-named
businesses; each was checked and ruled out:

- **Luna Physical Therapy** (getluna.com) — in-home physical therapy, insurance-billed, pays PTs a
  fixed rate per visit. Same general "we bill insurance, provider gets a set rate" shape as a doula
  billing platform, but a different clinical field entirely (physical therapy, not birth work).
  This is the most prominent "Luna" in healthcare billing and the likely source of confusion if
  someone half-remembered a "Luna bills insurance for a home-visit provider" model and misapplied
  it to doula care. Ruled out: not a birth-worker service.
  (https://www.getluna.com/how-luna-works, read 2026-09-16)
- **LunaBill** (lunabill.com) — AI revenue-cycle-automation product sold to hospitals and large RCM
  firms, not to individual doulas or birth workers. Ruled out: wrong customer and wrong scale.
  (https://lunabill.com/, read 2026-09-16)
- **Luna Health** (lunahealth.solu•tions) — connects patients to drug copay-assistance and coupon
  programs. Ruled out: pharmacy savings, not claims billing. (https://www.lunahealth.solu•tions/,
  read 2026-09-16)
- **La Luna Doula Services**, **Lune Doula Services**, **Luna Doula** (lunadoula.org), **Luna Care
  Studio**, **Luna Midwifery**, **Luna Natal Doula Care** — all individual doula or midwifery
  practices (single providers or small local practices) that see their own clients and, in some
  cases, take insurance for their own care. None of these process claims on behalf of other
  doulas as a third-party service. Ruled out: each one is a birth worker, not a billing vendor for
  birth workers. (each read 2026-09-16 — e.g. https://www.lunadoula.org/,
  https://lunamidwifery.com/)
- **Luna Birth School** — a birth-worker training and certification program, not a billing
  service. Ruled out. (https://lunabirthschool.com/, read 2026-09-16)

Confidence: high that no birth-worker claims-processing company is actually named "Luna" as of
2026-09-16. The search covered the direct name, "doula"/"birth worker"/"midwife" combined queries,
and a company-database-style query, and every hit resolved to one of the categories above. The
company that matches the brief's *description* (files Medicaid and private claims for a network of
independent doulas, handles credentialing, pays out on a set cycle) is **Loula** (joinloula.com), a
New York-based, Y Combinator-backed (batch W23) startup currently focused on California Medi-Cal
doulas. This is very likely what "Luna" was meant to refer to, but it is not what "Luna" literally
names, so it needs operator confirmation before it goes on the page — the household rule that a
public comparison against a named competitor must match that competitor's own published pricing
exactly means getting the company's name wrong is disqualifying on its own.
