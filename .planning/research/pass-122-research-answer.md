# The Micah Jones Consulting Site: A Research-Backed Design Recommendation

**Bottom line up front:** A safe, text-led consultant site cannot do what you want — the strongest sites of 2025–2026 that make a skeptical stranger lean in within seconds are built on *typography, numbers, and motion as the primary interface*, not paragraphs. For you specifically, the winning move is to turn your five outcomes into an unmissable "ledger" of big numbers with one interactive proof artifact that demonstrates you actually build AI, rendered in a distinctive editorial-plus-technical system that no template or AI generator would produce.

## TL;DR
- **Text is the problem, not the solution.** Your bland/wordy drafts fail because prose is the exact medium buyers skim past; replace it with oversized kinetic typography and animated number blocks that state $20M+, four exits, $14M/$1.2M, $3M/11 awards, and 800K impressions before anyone reads a sentence.
- **Recommended ethos: "Proof, not prose."** A monochrome editorial spine (huge type, generous negative space, one accent color) that reads as premium and fast on a phone, spiked with one genuinely interactive "system" artifact that proves the AI-engineering claim — this fits a one-person, high-trust, big-numbers, few-pictures business better than any photography-driven or logo-wall approach.
- **Three distinct directions follow** (The Ledger; The Operating System; Kinetic Editorial), a clear pick (The Ledger, with one interactive proof module), an explicit shot/asset list to fill your image gap, and the current 2026 "AI-slop" tells to avoid (Inter, indigo→purple gradients, three equal cards, centered everything).

## 2. The design ethos I recommend: "Proof, not prose"

In plain words: **your website should behave like a pitch deck's money slide, not like a brochure.** Every scroll should surface a piece of evidence — a number, a before/after, a working artifact — and the words should be captions to that evidence, not the evidence itself.

Why this fits a one-person, high-trust business with big numbers and few pictures:

- **You are the product, and your product is judgment plus outcomes.** You have no logos and no testimonials, but you have rare, specific numbers ($20M+, four exits, $14M revenue / $1.2M average deal, $3M / 11 awards, 800K impressions, 1→3 to 5→10 bookings). Numbers this good are wasted inside sentences. Set them at display scale and they become the design.
- **Skeptical buyers trust restraint.** In an AI-saturated web, disciplined visual craft functions as a trust signal — a considered monochrome system with real typographic craft reads as "senior operator," while a colorful, busy template reads as agency or amateur. (This is a widely-shared view among 2026 design commentators rather than a measured statistic; treat it as informed opinion.)
- **Few images is an advantage, not a liability, if you commit to type.** basement.studio, Vercel, By-Kin and Aristide Benoist prove a near-imageless site can be the most premium thing in the category. You stop competing on photography (which you don't have) and compete on typography and motion (which a browser can do infinitely).
- **You must *show* the AI claim, not assert it.** Anyone can write "I build AI systems." Almost no one embeds a working, interactive artifact that proves it. That single move (Ciechanowski-style) does more for your credibility than any amount of copy — and it's on-brand because you actually build.

The honest answer to your question: **a text-led site cannot make a stranger believe you in seconds.** What replaces the text is (a) big-number kinetic typography, (b) tight before→after data pairs, and (c) one interactive proof artifact. Words shrink to labels and one-line captions.

## 3. Three genuinely different directions

### Direction A — "The Ledger" (editorial monochrome, numbers-as-hero)

- **First five seconds on a phone:** A near-black or paper-white full-bleed screen. Your name and one positioning line at the top in large type; then, immediately, a single enormous number — **$20M+** — with a one-line caption ("in revenue behind the work"). No photo, no nav clutter, no gradient. A thin monospace label reads "Independent operator — Oakland, CA."
- **Typography:** A high-contrast display serif for the numbers and headlines (e.g., **Editorial New** by Pangram Pangram, or **GT Sectra** by Grilli Type) paired with a clean grotesque for labels/body (e.g., **Söhne** by Klim, or free alternative **General Sans** / **Geist**). Monospace for metadata (**Geist Mono** or **JetBrains Mono**). The serif-display-plus-mono pairing is itself an anti-AI signal.
- **Color:** Strict monochrome — warm near-black (#171717 or a warmer #1a1310) on paper-white (#fafafa), plus exactly **one** accent pulled from something real (e.g., a single confident amber or oxblood), used only on active/hover states. Depth from film-grain SVG texture at ~0.06 opacity, never from gradients.
- **Motion:** Restrained and directed. Numbers count up once on entry (respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`); section transitions use a weighted smooth scroll (Lenis) with GSAP ScrollTrigger reveals; hairline rules draw in. No WebGL required, so it's bulletproof on mobile.
- **Five case studies, minimal reading:** Each becomes a **stat card** with a giant primary number, a before→after pair, and one sentence.
  - *Guardicore:* "$14M revenue · $1.2M average enterprise deal" + "Repositioned from honeypots → east-west visibility; sat in the deals." → acquired by Akamai in October 2021 (Akamai reported the purchase at $610.4M in cash in its FY2021 10-K), a company-scale outcome behind your line.
  - *RFP engine:* an animated bar moving "1 in 8 → 1 in 4" close rate, and "$3M signed · 11 awards."
  - *AI content engine:* a sparkline climbing "few thousand → 800,000 impressions/mo," plus "1 rough video → a week of content; 1 income stream → 4."
  - *Birth worker practice:* "1–3 → 5–10 bookings/month."
  - *ORDANI:* "Hundreds of $ per client kept" with a small animated flow diagram (claims service vs. ORDANI).
- **Reference sites:** vercel.com, by-kin.com, linear.app, niniaazzopardi.com.

### Direction B — "The Operating System" (dark technical, live-system feel)

- **First five seconds on a phone:** A dark #0B0B0C terminal-like surface with a subtle blueprint grid. A blinking cursor types your positioning line; a compact "system status" panel shows four modules — Positioning/GTM, AI Engineering, Product, ORDANI — as if they were running services. The four exits appear as a ticker.
- **Typography:** Mono-led. **Geist Mono** or **Berkeley Mono** for headings/labels; a neutral grotesque (**Geist**, **Söhne**) for body. This directly encodes "I build systems."
- **Color:** Near-black canvas, off-white text, one electric accent (a restrained green or blue) for live/active states; optionally a two-color scheme like Cerebrium's #172B76/#902177 for data-viz moments.
- **Motion:** This is where you *prove the AI claim.* One signature interactive artifact — e.g., a live, in-browser mini "content engine" or "RFP scorer" diagram the visitor can nudge (sliders/toggles) to watch outputs change, in the Ciechanowski explorable-explanation tradition. Elsewhere, shader/WebGL accents (React Three Fiber) kept to hero and one section only, with a static fallback.
- **Five case studies, minimal reading:** Rendered as **interactive diagrams / screen recordings.** Guardicore as an animated "honeypots → east-west visibility" schematic; AI content engine as a node graph (1 video → 4 channels) with the impressions counter; ORDANI as a data-flow showing dollars retained; RFP engine as an interactive close-rate slider; birth-worker practice as a small calendar filling from 1–3 to 5–10.
- **Reference sites:** cerebrium.ai, vercel.com, iglooinc.com, ciechanow.ski.

### Direction C — "Kinetic Editorial" (maximal type, motion-forward)

- **First five seconds on a phone:** Full-bleed display type at ~14–20vw — a single word or your name — that compresses/expands with scroll (variable-font weight/width mapped to scroll position). The Tel Aviv 4-second animation plays muted in a masked shape behind the type for one beat, then type takes over.
- **Typography:** A characterful variable display face (e.g., **Clash Display** by Indian Type Foundry / free on Fontshare, or a licensed **PP Editorial**/**Migra**) driving kinetic headlines; a quiet grotesque for the few captions.
- **Color:** Poster-like — one bold ground (deep ink, or a single saturated brand color sampled from something real) with high-contrast type; imagery used sparingly and treated (duotone/grain).
- **Motion:** Heavy but choreographed — kinetic typography (GSAP SplitText), scroll-driven "scrollytelling" case studies where each number flies in and lands, page transitions via the View Transitions API. Highest craft, highest risk on performance and reduced-motion.
- **Five case studies, minimal reading:** Cinematic scroll sequences — the number is the hero, animating on entry (e.g., "$14M" assembling from fragments; "1 in 8 → 1 in 4" as a kinetic swap). Almost no prose; captions only.
- **Reference sites:** obys.agency, matvoyce.tv, unseen.co, basement.studio.

**How the directions differ:** A is *quiet and evidentiary* (numbers do the talking, minimal motion, safest on mobile); B is *technical and interactive* (proves you build AI via a working artifact, dark/system aesthetic); C is *loud and expressive* (kinetic type as spectacle, biggest punch, biggest risk). They are not three flavors of one idea — different aesthetics, different primary media (numbers vs. interactive systems vs. kinetic type), different risk profiles.

## 4. My pick — and what would make it fail

**Pick: Direction A ("The Ledger") as the spine, with exactly one interactive proof module borrowed from Direction B.**

Why: A best solves your actual constraints simultaneously — few images, big numbers, high trust, must work beautifully on a phone, must not look AI-built. It is the most mobile-robust (no mandatory WebGL), the fastest to load, the easiest for a solo founder to maintain, and the most credibly "senior operator." The one borrowed B module (a small interactive artifact that proves the AI-engineering claim) supplies the "design skill on show / real craft" punch and differentiates you from every other monochrome consultant site. This hybrid gets you visual punch *and* trust without betting the whole site on heavy motion.

**What would make it fail:**
- **Timidity.** If the numbers aren't genuinely huge (true full-viewport scale), it collapses back into the bland, tasteful site you're trying to escape. The number *is* the hero; treat it like one.
- **Monochrome without craft.** Monochrome only reads as premium if the typography, spacing and micro-timing are impeccable. Sloppy type or default spacing makes minimalism look empty, not expensive.
- **The interactive module being fake or gratuitous.** If the "proof" artifact is decorative rather than a real, nudge-able demonstration, skeptical technical buyers will clock it instantly and it backfires. It must do something real.
- **Motion that janks on a mid-range Android.** Jurors and buyers test on real phones; a counter or scroll effect that drops frames destroys the premium impression. Budget for 60fps and a clean `prefers-reduced-motion` path.
- **Reintroducing prose.** The failure mode you already know — if paragraphs creep back in to "explain," you lose the five-second believability.

## 5. What to cut, and what to create or shoot

**Cut:**
- Long "About"/philosophy paragraphs, mission statements, and any "Why work with me" prose block. Replace with numbers + one-line captions.
- The instinct to add a client-logo wall or testimonials you don't have. Absence is fine; *don't* fake or imply them.
- Any three-equal-cards services section (see §6). Present your offers as a simple, asymmetric price ladder: bespoke (scoped on a call), then **$500 / $2,500 / $7,500** fixed packages.
- Stock photography, illustration blobs, and gradients entirely.

**Create / shoot (with rough solo-founder effort):**
- **Photo/video session (half a day, ~$300–$800 with a local photographer) — LOW/MED effort.** You only need a handful of frames because the site is type-led:
  - 2–3 high-resolution, high-contrast environmental portraits of you *working* (whiteboard, screen, in-discussion) — the "operator mid-thought," not a smiling headshot. Shoot against a plain wall for easy duotone/grain treatment.
  - A short (5–10s) loop of you working/writing on a screen, to be masked behind type (Direction A/C hero beat).
  - Re-use your existing Tel Aviv photo + 4s animation as a single, treated hero accent — it's enough.
- **The interactive proof module (the important one) — MED/HIGH effort, but you build AI, so it's in your wheelhouse.** Build one small, real, in-browser artifact that demonstrates a system you actually made — e.g., a stripped-down "content engine" that takes a prompt and shows the 1-video-to-4-channels fan-out, or an "RFP scorer" slider. Use synthetic/dummy data only.
- **ORDANI without real client data — MED effort.** Do *not* screenshot real PHI. Instead: (a) build a **designed UI recreation** with fully synthetic data (fake doula/client names), captured as clean device mockups; and/or (b) a short **screen recording** of a synthetic workflow; and/or (c) an **animated diagram** of the "hundreds of dollars kept per client vs. a claims service" flow. The doula-with-client photo you have can anchor the ORDANI section.
- **Data-visualization assets — LOW/MED effort.** Design (in Figma, then animate with SVG/GSAP or Rive/Lottie): the close-rate bar (1-in-8 → 1-in-4), the impressions sparkline (→800K), the bookings calendar (1–3 → 5–10), the exits timeline (SurveyMonkey 2018 → Postmates 2020 → Guardicore 2021 → Neuton.AI 2025). These *are* your imagery.
- **Screen recordings of your AI systems — LOW effort.** Even 5–15s clips of real tools running (with sensitive data blurred) add authenticity that stock never can.

## 6. The 2026 "AI-generated / templated" tells to avoid

Designers and critics in 2025–2026 converge on a specific fingerprint. Avoid all of these:

- **The Inter default (and Roboto/Open Sans/Arial/Helvetica).** These are the default fonts in AI tools and component libraries; using them signals "nobody chose anything." Choose a distinctive display face + a considered grotesque + a mono. (Per multiple 2026 audits, changing the typeface is the single highest-impact fix.)
- **The indigo→purple gradient ("the Purple Problem").** The single loudest AI tell. Its origin is documented: on Aug 7, 2025, Tailwind CSS creator Adam Wathan posted (1.5M views) *"I'd like to formally apologize for making every button in Tailwind UI bg-indigo-500 five years ago, leading to every AI generated UI on earth also being indigo,"* and the "Purple Problem" label was popularized in Alan West's DEV Community write-up. Delete every linear/radial/mesh gradient; use flat color + grain/texture for depth instead.
- **The startup palette (purple/indigo/blue) and timid, low-commitment color.** Sample your palette from something real; hold one temperature (don't mix warm and cool greys); use exactly one accent.
- **Three equal cards in a row with icon-heading-two-lines.** The most generic AI layout that exists (the old Tailwind grid demo). Use a bento grid, an icon list, or an asymmetric zig-zag instead — and never repeat it.
- **Everything centered and symmetrical.** "Symmetry is what you get when nobody made a decision." Offset copy, anchor elements off-center.
- **Uniform component sizing** — identical 16px radius, identical padding, identical card heights everywhere. Real hierarchy comes from intentional variation.
- **Vague aspirational headlines** ("Build the future," "Your all-in-one platform," "Scale without limits"). Be specific like Stripe ("Financial infrastructure for the internet"); your numbers make specificity easy.
- **AI-generated illustrations / 3D blobs / stock "diverse team at a laptop."** They read as plastic and too-smooth; use real (even minimal) photography, real screen recordings, or designed data-viz.
- **Everything-in-threes and cards-everywhere** (border + shadow + white background on every element). Vary group sizes; reserve cards for where they earn their place.
- **No `prefers-reduced-motion` path.** A missing or jarring reduced-motion fallback is an amateur tell; craftspeople build a graceful one (Lenis and GSAP both support it natively).

On the business stakes: it's worth noting the most-cited "38% of people stop engaging with an unattractive site" figure comes from a 2015 Adobe survey, not fresh 2026 research — treat it as directional, not current. The durable, defensible point is simpler: in a web where anyone can generate a median-looking site in minutes, deliberate differentiation *is* the conversion strategy, because sameness gives a skeptical buyer no reason to stay.

### Build-stack notes (Next.js/React)
Lenis for smooth scroll (respects reduced-motion; pair with GSAP ScrollTrigger); Motion (formerly Framer Motion) or GSAP for reveals and counters; CSS scroll-driven animations for lightweight effects; View Transitions API for page transitions; variable fonts for kinetic type without file bloat; Rive or Lottie for the animated diagrams; React Three Fiber only for the single hero/proof moment, with a static fallback and a device check (`if (window.devicePixelRatio < 2 && window.innerWidth < 768) return;`) to protect low-end mobile. Target 60fps on a mid-range Android and sub-2.5s load; screenshot every hero statically to confirm the frame is strong before motion is added.

## Caveats
- **Verify each URL and its live state before you cite or link it.** Galleries reshuffle and personal/studio sites redesign frequently; I sourced these from live 2025–2026 gallery listings, but confirm each loads and still matches the described "move" at build time. `niniaazzopardi.com` and `marketbuildr.com` were the clearest verified solo-operator proof-by-numbers examples; several others are designers/studios chosen because they solve your *constraint* (type/motion over photography), not because they are consultants.
- **The exact combination you want is rare.** Solo operator + high-trust B2B + big revenue numbers + genuinely no photography/logos + high craft is an uncommon intersection; most striking solo sites either lean on marquee client *names* (Aristide Benoist) or still include a portrait/logo wall (Ninia Azzopardi). This is precisely why executing "The Ledger" well would make you stand out rather than blend in.
- **A few figures in the wider design-trend commentary should be treated as informed opinion, not hard data** — notably the "visual craft as trust signal" framing and the recycled 38% engagement statistic. The design recommendations themselves rest on the consistent, well-documented convergence of 2026 practitioners on the AI-slop tells in §6.
- **Use only your true facts.** Every number in the designs above maps to a fact you supplied; do not let a designer or copywriter round up, invent testimonials, imply clients from the exit companies, or use real ORDANI client data. Synthetic data and treated versions of your two real photos are the correct path.
- **Bruno Simon date correction:** his driving portfolio's Awwwards honors are from 2019 (Site of the Month Nov 2019, Site of the Year 2019), not 2026 — some secondary blogs misattribute a "2026" date; it remains a valid boundary-case reference regardless.
