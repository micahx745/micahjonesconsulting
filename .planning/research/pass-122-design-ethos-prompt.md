# Prompt for a Claude chat (Fable, research on): the best design ethos for micahjonesconsulting.com

Written 2026-09-18 after the operator rejected the Pass-121 Direction C mocks on sight ("bland, word heavy,
weak ... would not draw someone in"). Deliberately carries no design rules from the current site, so the
research does not return the design already built. Prompt 2 is optional and comes after prompt 1's answer.

---

## PROMPT 1 (paste this first)

I need you to research and recommend the best possible design for my personal consulting website. Please do
real research: look at the strongest websites being made right now, not what you already assume. I want the
design that makes a stranger stop, lean in, and believe me within seconds. I do NOT want a safe, tasteful,
text-heavy consultant site. My current attempts keep coming out bland and wordy, so push hard.

**Who I am.** Micah Jones, an independent operator in Oakland, CA. I do three things for companies:
positioning and go-to-market, AI engineering (I build AI systems myself), and product building. I also founded
and built ORDANI, a HIPAA-compliant CRM for birth workers (doulas, midwives), in beta with paying users.

**What's true (use only these; never invent clients, numbers or testimonials):**
- $20M+ in revenue behind my work.
- Four companies I worked inside reached an exit: SurveyMonkey (IPO, 2018), Postmates (acquired by Uber,
  $2.65B, 2020), Guardicore (acquired by Akamai, 2021), Neuton.AI (technology acquired by Nordic
  Semiconductor, 2025).
- Guardicore: I found what buyers were actually paying for, moved the pitch from honeypots to east-west
  visibility, and sat in the deals. $14M in revenue, $1.2M average enterprise deal.
- An RFP engine I built for an award-winning author and consultant: $3M in signed contracts across eleven
  awards; close rate from one in eight to one in four.
- An AI content engine for a social activist: from a few thousand impressions a month to a peak of 800,000;
  one rough video becomes the week's content; one income stream became four.
- A birth worker's practice: bookings from one to three a month to five to ten.
- ORDANI: birth workers keep hundreds of dollars per client that a claims service would take.
- Offers: bigger engagements scoped on a call, plus fixed packages at $500, $2,500 and $7,500.

**Who has to be drawn in.** Founders and leaders of B2B and software companies who need growth, sharper
positioning, or AI built properly; people who hire one senior operator instead of an agency. They skim, they
are skeptical of consultants, and they have seen a thousand templates.

**What I have to work with, honestly.** Few images: one real photo of me mid-discussion at a working session
in Tel Aviv (plus a 4-second animated version of it), one photo of a doula with a client for ORDANI, and my
own words. No client logos I can use, no testimonials, no ORDANI screenshots yet (they hold real client data).
If the right design needs imagery, video, or anything else I don't have, tell me exactly what to create or
shoot. That is a fair answer.

**What I want people to feel.** Visual punch. Design skill on show. Real motion and craft. It must not look
built by AI or from a template. It has to work beautifully on a phone. The site is built in modern web tech
(Next.js/React), so anything a modern browser can do is on the table.

**What I need back:**
1. Your research: 15 to 25 real, current websites that do this kind of job brilliantly (studios, independent
   designers, operators and consultants, editorial and product sites, award winners; Awwwards, Godly,
   SiteInspire and similar are good places to start), each with its URL and one line on the specific move
   that makes it work.
2. The design ethos you recommend for me, in plain words, and why it fits a one-person, high-trust business
   with big numbers and few pictures.
3. Three genuinely different directions, each with: what a visitor sees on a phone in the first five seconds,
   the typography, colour and imagery, how motion is used, how the five case studies are told with far less
   reading, and two or three real reference sites. Make them different from each other, not three flavours
   of one idea.
4. Your pick of the three, and what would make it fail.
5. What I should cut, and what I should create or shoot to make it work.
6. The common tells that make a site look AI-generated or templated in 2026, so I can avoid them.

Be opinionated. If the honest answer is that a text-led site cannot do what I want, say so and tell me what
replaces the text.

## PROMPT 2 (optional, paste after its answer to prompt 1)

My current site follows these design rules. Many came with the tool setup the site was started on, not from
a deliberate choice. Now that you have your recommendation: which of these rules does it break, which of
them are holding my site back, and which (if any) are worth keeping and why? Answer rule by rule.

- One accent colour only (a copper orange), everything else warm cream paper and near-black ink; case
  studies open on a dark band.
- Three fonts: Bricolage Grotesque (display), Hanken Grotesk (body), JetBrains Mono (labels only; mono is
  banned for anything else).
- Type and photographs or screenshots only: no illustration, no icons, no 3D, no stock imagery.
- One signature motion on the site (a title that settles in), plus a page-to-page dim. Nothing may pin,
  stick, parallax-scroll, or follow the cursor. Entrance motion runs once, 400ms or less, and nothing loops.
- No logo walls, "trusted by" bars, testimonial carousels, or animated counters (one exception exists on
  the home page).
- Copy: first person, sentences short, specific numbers only, at most one em-dash per page, a 30-word
  banned-words list.
