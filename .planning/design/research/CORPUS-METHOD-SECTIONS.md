# CORPUS METHOD SECTIONS — what sits in the process / how-it-works / capabilities panel

**Leg A.** Question answered: the operator's "How I work" rail (`01 Diagnose / 02 Build /
03 Position`, each with a panel) needs panel content. He rejected a photograph of himself and a
photograph of a whiteboard. This walks every build in the client-work corpus and records what is
actually IN that slot.

## 0. The finding that answers the rejection directly

**0 of 14 builds put a photograph of the founder, the team, or the workspace in the process /
how-it-works / capabilities panel.** Not one.

Faces appear in exactly three places across the whole corpus, and none of them is a method panel:
a **testimonial** card (`cora §6.3` eight named quotes with headshots; `hyperbolic §6.6`;
`growthloop §6.7` a named person with a headshot inside a proof card), a dedicated **team**
section (`dragonfly §6.5`, where the four partner portraits are graded so dark they "read as
texture until you look"; `lore §6.12`, three founders inside a WebGL ring), and an author byline.
A whiteboard, a desk, an office, a sticky-note wall, or a hand-at-work photograph appears **zero
times in 14 builds**.

His instinct to reject both is corroborated by the corpus, not merely a taste call. What goes in
the slot instead falls into seven patterns, below.

## 2. The seven patterns, with the exact mechanism and the translation

### A. The abstract system diagram, drawn in live type — `tambo §6.6`
**Mechanism.** Five rounded boxes (two mint, two dark, one nested), arranged by a single CSS
`transform` in 3D perspective — `tambo §7`: "The How-It-Works boxes are rotated in 3D space via CSS
transform, not an image." Box titles in the serif at 300 weight, subtitles in small sans. Hand-curved
SVG arrows connect them, each carrying a **mono capsule label riding the curve**. It occupies a full
screen with ~300px of silence under it. Motion: none. Frame: none — no border, no card; it sits on
the page's own hatched ground.
**For him.** This is `tambo §10`'s own verdict: "He **already owns the source material**. The book
has nine hand-drawn pages; one of them is the page-6 diagram the `<WallChart />` already draws. This
is the same move at hero scale." The whiteboard service diagram he wanted to photograph is the same
object — **redrawn** it becomes this; **photographed** it becomes the thing he rejected. Arrow labels
carry the sentences that would otherwise be three paragraphs: what a client hands him, what comes
back.

### B. The real artifact, cropped, in one soft frame — `cora §6.4–6.7`, `growthloop §6.13`, `hyperbolic §6.3`
**Mechanism.** `cora §7`: six `position: sticky` full-viewport panels, classnames verbatim
`sticky top-0 h-[100vh] flex items-center justify-center`. Copy holds still on the left; the right
holds one white card with a large radius and a soft shadow, **no device chrome, no browser bar, no
laptop mockup**, floating directly on the section's ground colour. The content is the product's own
real output — an email with a real sender, subject and body. `growthloop §6.13` runs the same card
on flat blue. `hyperbolic §6.3` uses a muted looping `<video>` of the product creating an instance.
Caption style: none — the panel is unlabelled; the left-hand copy is the caption.
**For him.** Three panels, three real artifacts, no captions: **one page of the audit memo** cropped
to a single finding (client name redacted — `oreo §10`'s "NAME WITHHELD BY REQUEST" is the honest
treatment and reads as *more* credible than a fake one); **one screen of the shipped HIPAA CRM**;
**one page of the RFP engine's actual output**. Same frame for all three, same radius, same ground.
`growthloop §11` gives the rule that makes this survivable at his scale: "the card should be built so
that a **missing image is a legitimate variant rather than a hole**."

### C. One held abstract drawing while the list advances — `hyperbolic §6.4`, `sharplink §6.3`
**Mechanism.** `hyperbolic §10`: "a left column that holds a single piece of art in place, and a
right column that scrolls through three named audiences, each with a mono eyebrow, one sentence of
promise, three sentences of mechanism, and **its own CTA verb**." The held art is not an
illustration — it is nested dashed parallelograms, pure geometry. `sharplink §6.3` runs the identical
rail with five propositions and grades the ink: the block you are reading is at full ink, the ones
above and below sit back. Reveal offsets are declared in markup: `data-gsap='{"x":0,"y":"10%"}'` on
the media, `{"y":"30%"}` on the copy, so the picture lands before the claim.
**Cost.** `growthloop §10`: "one CSS property and an opacity class. `position: sticky` on the left
column, and `opacity: .48` to `1` with `transition: opacity 300ms`. No GSAP required."
**For him.** This is the rail he already has. `hyperbolic §10` also supplies the free rule he is
currently at risk of failing: **one verb per step, never the same verb three times.**

### D. Typographic-only panels — `esther §6.5`, `tambo §6.7`, `hyperbolic §6.7`, `dragonfly §6.6`, `psyop §6.2`, `growthloop §6.5`, `oreo §10`
**Mechanism, five variants.**
- *Labelled proofs:* `esther §6.5` — one process claim, split into three mono-caps labels each with
  one sentence. Nothing else on screen.
- *Ordinal grid:* `hyperbolic §6.7` — six items `01`–`06`, two columns, mono eyebrow, short head,
  two lines of body, no icons.
- *Chips:* `tambo §6.7` — ten uppercase mono capability chips with a small arrow, orbiting the head.
- *Section furniture as the graphic:* `dragonfly §6.6` — accent ordinal `04`, section name, a
  `SEC—04` § code sitting on the hairline, a **count** (`09`) in 10px mono, then names at display
  scale with a 10px mono category beside them.
- *Provenance micro-line:* `oreo §10` — claim, initials mark, and a caps micro-line naming where it
  came from. "Four typographic elements, no photo, no logo, no video."
**For him.** All of it is already legal in the House Lights constitution — JetBrains Mono is cleared
for "labels, § codes and data," and `dragonfly §10` notes "the `SEC—04` idiom *is* the § code." The
count is the honest move: `03` under `HOW I WORK`, `07` under the receipts. `dragonfly §11`'s caution
applies — "A text index is honest precisely because it is countable, and a padded one is the fastest
way to lose the reader who counts."

### E. Numbers as the graphic — `viture §6.3`, `provable §6.2`, `growthloop §6.7`
**Mechanism.** `viture §10`: "a headline, a row of comparative numbers set at display size with a
gradient clipped into the glyphs, and one asterisked footnote naming the baseline. No chart, no icon,
no photograph, no logo — the numbers ARE the graphic." `provable §10`: equal cards, "a mono uppercase
label, one figure at 24–32px," borders at 4% ink, "nothing on the page is bigger than the numbers."
**The two free rules.** `viture §10`: the **footnote naming what the number is measured against**,
"which turns a boast into a claim someone could check"; and the **placement — proof at position 3 of
11, before a single feature is explained.**
**For him.** His seven receipts are the raw material. A step whose panel is `$150K recovered` at
display size with `*Compared to the 12 months before engagement` under it is a stronger panel than
any picture — and it costs nothing.

### F. The prerendered object — `sharplink §6.3`, `viture §6.3`, `esther §6.8`, `lore §6.12`. **NOT AVAILABLE.**
`sharplink §11`: two studio-rendered CGI films, "five figures of asset production before a line of
CSS was written… There is no cheap version." `viture §11`: 336-frame render sequence — "Do not
attempt a frame sequence with a photograph of yourself." `esther §11`: DRACO-compressed GLTF plus a
lighting rig matched to a photo shoot. `lore §11`: "a cheap 3D word reads worse than no 3D at all."
Named here only so it is not drifted into.

### F′. The named-failure-mode list — `looped §6.4`
**Mechanism.** Five specific pains, each with a name, in the buyer's own language rather than one
abstract category claim. `looped §10`: "name the five failure modes his buyers actually live in, in
their words."
**For him.** This is the honest panel for `01 Diagnose`, and it needs no asset at all.

### G/H. Nothing — `dragonfly §6.2`, `lore`, `bad-omens`, `psyop`
`dragonfly §6.2` is the strongest instance: the "what we do" section is **268px on a 13,726px page**
— one sentence, one label, one paragraph, no panel. Four of fourteen builds ship no method panel
whatsoever. An empty right column is a legitimate corpus-supported answer for any step whose artifact
he does not yet have; it is also `psyop §6.2`'s entire homepage strategy, where the proof is withheld
until the visitor reaches for it.

## 3. Recommendation, ranked

The rail has three panels. The ranking is by **which pattern belongs in the slot**, with the reason a
jury would give and the reason a buyer would give, because those differ and both must hold.

### 1. The real artifact, cropped, in one soft frame — pattern B (`cora §6.5`, `growthloop §6.13`)
Panel `01 Diagnose` = one page of the **written audit memo**, cropped to a single finding, client
redacted. Panel `02 Build` = one screen of the **shipped HIPAA CRM**. Panel `03 Position` = the
**RFP engine's real output**, or the positioning line set as type. Same card, same radius, same
ground, no device chrome, no caption.
- *Jury:* it is the only panel type in the corpus that cannot be bought. `cora`'s card works because
  the email inside it is real, with a real sender and a real subject; the frame is generic and the
  content is not. A jury reads authored evidence, not an asset purchase.
- *Buyer:* they see the thing they will receive, in the order they will receive it, before they have
  to trust anything he says about it. `growthloop §11`'s rule keeps him honest: build the card so a
  missing image is a legitimate variant.

### 2. The book's own diagram, redrawn in live type — pattern A (`tambo §6.6`)
Page 6 of the 69-page field manual — already the `<WallChart />` subject — rebuilt as boxes plus SVG
arrows plus mono capsule labels, at panel scale, for whichever step is the method's spine.
- *Jury:* type + boxes + SVG, one CSS transform, no illustration, no icon kit, no stock, no 3D —
  exactly the House Lights ban list, satisfied. It is *made*, and that reads at a glance.
- *Buyer:* the arrow labels do the explaining that three paragraphs would. `tambo §10`: "the arrows
  between what a client hands him and what comes back — is the whole pitch for a $5K/mo engagement
  and it fits on one screen."
- **This is also the answer to the rejected whiteboard.** The content he wanted from that photograph
  survives; the photograph does not. Redraw it, do not shoot it.

### 3. Numbers as the graphic, with the baseline footnote — pattern E (`viture §6.3`, `provable §6.2`)
For any step where a receipt is stronger than a picture: the number at display size, a mono caps
label, and one asterisked line naming what it is measured against.
- *Jury:* `provable`'s discipline — "nothing on the page is bigger than the numbers" — is a whole
  design position stated in one rule, and it holds at 32px with no second font.
- *Buyer:* the footnote is the difference between a boast and a claim they could check. It is the
  cheapest credibility on this list.

### 4. One held abstract drawing while the list advances — pattern C (`hyperbolic §6.4`)
If he wants a single image for the whole rail rather than three: **one** redrawn diagram held in the
left column while `01 / 02 / 03` pass it.
- *Jury:* `hyperbolic §10` — "It costs **one** image. Not three… it will read as deliberate rather
  than thin."
- *Buyer:* each step gets a full screen of attention in sequence, with no comparison table. Bring the
  free rule with it: **three different verbs** (`Book the audit` / `Start the sprint` /
  `Buy the manual`), never one verb three times.

### 5. Typographic-only labelled proofs — pattern D (`esther §6.5`, `dragonfly §6.6`, `tambo §6.7`)
Mono caps label, one sentence, hairline, § code, and a **count**. No panel image at all.
- *Jury:* `dragonfly` ships this at the scale where they could most afford not to — ~200 portfolio
  names as plain text, zero logos. Restraint at that scale reads as a house rule, not a shortage.
- *Buyer:* it is the fastest section on the page to read, and the count tells them the size of the
  thing honestly before they have to ask.

### 6. The named-failure-mode list, for `01 Diagnose` — pattern F′ (`looped §6.4`)
Five named pains in the buyer's words, as the diagnose panel.
- *Jury:* specificity is the whole craft signal; five named failures beat one abstract claim.
- *Buyer:* they recognise themselves before he has claimed anything.

### 7. Nothing — pattern G (`dragonfly §6.2`, 268px)
A legitimate answer for any step whose artifact does not exist yet. Four of fourteen builds do this.
- *Jury:* an empty column that is clearly a decision beats a filled one that is clearly a stopgap.
- *Buyer:* nothing to disbelieve.

### Rejected, with the citation
- **A photograph of him in the panel.** 0 of 14. Faces in this corpus live in testimonial cards
  (`cora §6.3`, `growthloop §6.7`) and a dedicated team section (`dragonfly §6.5`) — never in the
  method slot. His `/about` portrait mount is the correct home for the one portrait.
- **A photograph of the whiteboard.** 0 of 14. Redraw it (pattern A) or drop it.
- **A render, a 3D object, or a CGI film.** `sharplink §11`, `viture §11`, `esther §11`, `lore §11`
  all name this as budget with no cheap version.
- **Short clips of him in rooms, as the panel.** `psyop §11` is explicit about why the hover-reel
  works there — "25 years of Netflix, Nike and Google broadcast animation sitting behind the type…
  the same interaction with generic B-roll reads as a screensaver." Room footage is a footer or an
  `/about` asset, not a method panel. `viture §6.4` shows the one legal clip form: a short **labelled
  micro-clip of the artifact doing one verb** — a CRM screen completing an intake, not a person
  talking.
- **A logo wall or a "trusted by" bar in the rail.** `hyperbolic §11`, `viture §11`, `dragonfly §10`
  — he has no logos, and the corpus's best build ships none on purpose.
