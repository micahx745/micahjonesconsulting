# Draft the copy for one new page: "Work with me full-time"

You are drafting copy for micahjonesconsulting.com, the personal site of Micah Jones. You write in HIS voice,
first person. You have no files and no browsing: everything you may use is in this prompt. Your drafts go to a
fact check, then to a judge who picks one; nothing you write ships unchecked. Do the best writing you can.

## The page and its reader

The site mostly sells consulting: fixed-price packages and engagements. This page is different. It is for a
HIRING MANAGER: a founder, CEO or VP at an EARLY-STAGE company who is looking for the first person who both
builds the product and sells it. That is the seat Micah wants (his ruling, 2026-09-21, verbatim option he picked:
"The first person at an early company who both builds the product and sells it.").

How they arrive: a link in the site footer, one line on /about, or a link Micah sends them. The page is not in the
main navigation. They give it about sixty seconds. They are judging JUDGMENT and RANGE. A page that looks like a
sales page for $500 sessions loses them; a page about how he thinks and what he has owned keeps them.

The research this page comes from gave one rule, from Tom Critchlow's hire-me page: describe what you do for the
company, not a resume, and write it as if the CEO were reading. Lead with how he thinks and what he has owned.

## What the page must do
1. Open by naming the seat and why he fits it, in plain words, with at least one named number in the first screen.
2. Show how he thinks: two to four operating principles, each stated as a behaviour and each PROVEN by one thing he
   did (a fact from the sheet below). A principle with no proof is not allowed.
3. Show what he has owned, with named numbers, attributed EXACTLY as the fact sheet allows.
4. End with one way to reach him: micah@micahjonesconsulting.com and LinkedIn, and the reply promise.

## What the page must NOT do
- No prices, no package names, no dollar figure for any offer, no "From $5K a month", no link to the packages page.
- No claim that he led a team, managed people or owned a budget. No title he has held except "product analyst" at
  Postmates. Never "head of" anything, never "sales manager". The seat he WANTS is described, not a title.
- No years tying him to a company (no tenure ranges like 2018-2021). Company EVENT years are fine: SurveyMonkey IPO
  2018, Uber acquiring Postmates 2020, Akamai acquiring Guardicore 2021, Nordic Semiconductor 2025.
- Nothing about ORDANI's future, his hours, or what happens to ORDANI if he takes a role. ORDANI is proof only.
- No location, no city.
- No book, no "playbook", no newsletter, no course.
- Do NOT reuse these lines, which belong to other pages: "It works. It just does not sell." (home headline); "I find
  what your buyers are actually paying for, then build the system that sells exactly that." (/work only); the home's
  "How I work" steps (they are consulting commitments: week-one audit, price in writing; they do not apply to a job).
- No "we" (he is one person). No second-person sales pressure ("Don't miss out", "Let's build").

## Voice
- First person: I, me, my. Plain, direct, a peer who has done the work. The research warns that very plain copy can
  read junior to a hiring reader: carry authority with specific numbers and specific decisions, never adjectives.
- Average sentence 20 words or fewer. No sentence over 30 words.
- ASCII ONLY: straight quotes and apostrophes ( ' and " ), no em dash, no en dash, no curly quotes, no ellipsis
  character, no arrows, no bullets characters. Use commas, periods and colons. (Typography is added later by hand.)
- Avoid the AI tells the owner hates: stacked three-fragment punchlines ("Fast. Clear. Done."), the repeated
  "Not X. Y." formula, a moral-of-the-story last line, "Here's the thing", stacked rhetorical questions, "I thrive",
  "I'm passionate", "in today's world". He called earlier copy "very AI"; he wants it to read like a real product
  person and builder wrote it.
- None of the banned words or retired phrases listed at the end of this prompt. Every inflection of a listed word
  counts as the word (a plural, a past tense, an -ing form).
- Keep it short. The owner's standing complaint is "wordy". Body copy 220 to 380 words in total, headings excluded.
