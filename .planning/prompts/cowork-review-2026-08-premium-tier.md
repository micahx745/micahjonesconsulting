## THE CENTRAL QUESTION — do not dodge this
The site is being asked to convert **three** audiences:

- **A. Solo/"vibe coder" builders** — shipping with AI, stuck between demo and production. They
  buy a $149 field manual and eventually advisory. They respond to lived specifics and
  recognition of their exact failure mode. They are repelled by corporate polish.
- **B. Enterprises** — procurement, security review, six-figure engagements. They respond to
  restraint, proof, institutional legibility. They are repelled by anything that reads scrappy
  or "indie".
- **C. Small businesses** — budget-conscious, need outcomes explained without jargon.

These pull in opposite directions. Your job is NOT to say "balance them". Choose one of:
1. **One surface can do it** — then show exactly how: which elements carry A, which carry B,
   and why a member of each audience is not repelled by the other's cues. Name the elements.
2. **It cannot** — then specify the architecture: what the shared spine is (identity, proof,
   voice), what splits by route, what each audience's entry point is, and what the homepage
   must therefore stop trying to do.

Whichever you choose, state the ONE sentence the homepage must land in 2 seconds, and who it
is aimed at. Ambiguity here is the most expensive thing on the site.

## "AI SLOP" — grade this concretely, not by vibe
The operator's word. Define it as these observable tells, and mark each PRESENT / ABSENT with
the element that triggered it:
- Copy rhythm: triads ("strategy, design, and code"), symmetric sentence pairs, em-dash-heavy
  clauses, every section opening with the same grammatical shape.
- Headline formula: abstract-noun + period ("Shipped, not pitched.") repeated as a system, so
  every section header sounds generated by the same template.
- Filler abstraction: sentences that survive being applied to any other consultant.
- Uniform density: every section the same length, same rhythm, no page that is deliberately
  short, quiet or strange.
- Decorative-but-meaningless motion or ornament; grain/noise used as a substitute for design.
- A palette that is "tasteful" in a way that resembles a template default rather than a chosen
  position.
For each PRESENT tell: quote it and rewrite it, in this operator's voice (first person, named
numbers, ≤25 words/sentence, at most one em-dash per page).

## "LUXURIOUS / PREMIUM" — the tier being graded against

## THE FOUR NAMED SURFACES — grade each, with computed values

**1. Color.** Current system: a scroll-driven set of "worlds" — terracotta `#9E3C25`, bone
`#ECE3D0`, petrol `#1A4548`, espresso `#2A1F18`, saffron `#C9982F` accent; case-study pages run
a warm near-black `#12100E` with bone ink. Questions you must answer: Is the palette a POSITION
or a preset? Does the world-switching read as authored or as decoration? Is saffron earning its
place? Does the terracotta read premium at full-bleed, or does it read like a 2024 template?
Would a restricted palette (fewer worlds, more contrast discipline) read more expensive? Sample
actual values; do not guess.

**2. Section headers.** Currently: a mono eyebrow + a large uppercase display line, repeated on
nearly every section, most of them abstract-noun+period. Grade the SYSTEM, not each instance:
is it a hierarchy or a drumbeat? Where should a header be smaller, absent, or replaced by
something that isn't a header at all?

**3. Typography.** Bricolage Grotesque (display) · Hanken Grotesk (body) · JetBrains Mono
(labels/data). Is the pairing distinctive or merely current? Does Bricolage at display weight
read authored or trendy? Is the mono doing real work or costume? Give a specific verdict:
keep / retune the scale / replace one face — and if replace, name the face and say why it fits
THIS operator. (Constraint: web-licensable, and performance is already tight — three families
is the ceiling.)

**4. Structure — page and case study.** The case studies are the proof layer, where a buyer
decides. Current shape: full-bleed title-card hero (word stack → one figure-bearing line) →
sticky meta rail → prose sections (The problem / What I did / Outcome) → artifact frames →
pull quote. Grade the SHAPE: does it read like evidence or like a blog post? What does a
$100K buyer need in the first screen that is not there? Is the prose-to-proof ratio right?
What should be a table, a number, an image, or cut entirely? Same question for `/services`
(comparative spec tables) and `/hire-me`.
