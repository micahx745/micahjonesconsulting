## The pivot — two buyers, two tiers

### Buyer A — companies hiring me

Companies considering me for **product, software engineering, or GTM roles**. Could be:
- Tech industry OR any industry (healthcare, finance, manufacturing, nonprofit)
- Full-time (W2 or 1099 senior IC / leadership)
- Fractional (fractional CTO, fractional head of product, fractional VP GTM)
- Senior contract (Director of Product, Staff Engineer, VP GTM on a defined engagement)

What this buyer needs to see immediately:
- The receipts (two exits, $20M revenue, named enterprise customers)
- That I can sit in a role and own outcomes, not just consult on the side
- Specific language that maps to job descriptions hiring managers actually post
- A way to express interest that doesn't require a full discovery call (could be: download my CV / portfolio PDF, email me, LinkedIn message)

### Buyer B — vibe coders who can't ship or can't get traction

Solo builders using AI tools (Cursor, Claude Code, Lovable, v0, Bolt) who:
- Think they have a solid idea
- Are stuck somewhere in build → launch → traction
- Cannot afford a $50K+ engagement with me
- CAN afford a $49–$299 PDF / playbook

Specific pain patterns to address in the offering:
- **Stuck mid-build:** "I got it 80% working and then the AI started breaking things every time I asked for changes. I don't know what to do next."
- **Can't launch:** "It works on my machine. I don't know how to deploy / secure / pay for hosting / make it real."
- **Launched, no traction:** "It's live. Nobody's using it. I don't know if it's the product, the messaging, the distribution, or all three."

This buyer doesn't need an enterprise-tier consultant. They need a **playbook from someone who actually shipped a real product solo using these same tools** — which I did with Ordani. The Ordani build is the proof.

### Tier 1 — the PDF

A paid digital product that:
- Solves a specific, named pain for Buyer B
- Is the most-credible answer the market has for that pain (because the author actually shipped a HIPAA-grade SaaS solo with AI tools)
- Costs somewhere between $49 and $299 (you propose where)
- Serves as an **appetizer for Buyer A and the full-engagement Buyer B** — somebody who buys the PDF, reads it, and realizes they need the operator behind it should have an obvious next step to book a call or apply for the engagement

Format is open — could be PDF, Notion template, video walkthrough, hybrid. You recommend.

### Tier 2 — the full engagement

What the site sells today: positioning & GTM, end-to-end product building, frontier AI engineering. Three services, four engagement shapes each (advisory / project / retainer / embedded). This stays — but the framing should be reachable from both the PDF audience (graduating up) AND from the corporate hiring audience (reachable as fractional / contract / FTE).

## Voice + brand rules the rewrite must obey

- **First-person operator voice.** "I" not "we." There is no team.
- **Sentence cap: 25 words.** Anything longer is a defect.
- **No buzzwords.** The site enforces a copy-lint ban-list of roughly thirty consultant-jargon / AI-slop terms. You know the register — verbs and nouns every SaaS landing page reaches for when it has nothing concrete to say. Avoid them.
- **No invented metrics.** Real receipts only.
- **Editorial restraint.** Reference voice: Monocle magazine, Bloomberg Businessweek feature page, WSJ feature insert. Not McKinsey deck, not SaaS landing page, not LinkedIn-influencer post.
- **Pain-led openings.** Lead with the buyer's pain in their own internal language, not with what the operator IS or DOES.
- **Evidence before claim.** Strong receipts first; let the buyer draw the conclusion. Don't say "I know what enterprise procurement pays for" — say "I shipped product deployed at TD Bank, Deutsche Bank, NIH, Peoples Natural Gas" and stop.

## What to produce

A single document structured exactly like this:

### § 1 — Positioning thesis (300 words max)
The one-sentence positioning that holds both buyers without bending either one out of shape. Then the supporting three to five sentences. Then the "why this is defensible" paragraph.

### § 2 — Buyer A copy direction
What language Buyer A is searching for. The signals they need to see in the first 5 seconds. The specific landing surface or section that should serve them on the site (new page? section of the home? a /hire-me page? a dedicated /resume?). 8–12 sentences max.

### § 3 — Buyer B copy direction
Same structure as § 2 but for the vibe-coder audience. 8–12 sentences max.

### § 4 — Tier 1 PDF product spec
- **Working title** (3 alternatives, pick one, explain why)
- **Format** (PDF / Notion / video / hybrid — recommend with reasoning)
- **Length** (pages or sections)
- **Price point** ($49 / $99 / $149 / $199 / $299 — recommend with market reasoning)
- **Table of contents** — chapter-by-chapter outline (8–15 sections)
- **Distribution / sales platform** — name a specific one with a one-sentence rationale
- **Lead-gen flow** — how does a PDF buyer become a discovery-call candidate? What's the upsell path?

### § 5 — Site-wide rewrite plan — page by page
For each existing surface (home hero, home Services row, home Operating Principles, home Shipped grid, /about, /services, /services/ai-engineering, /work, /work/guardicore, /work/ordani, /work/hr-equity-author):
- **Keep, modify, or kill** — your call
- If **modify**: name the specific lines/sections and propose the rewrite, in the operator voice
- If **kill**: explain what replaces it

### § 6 — New surfaces needed
What pages or sections need to be **added** for the pivot to land. Be specific — name the route, the H1, the structure, and the CTAs. Examples might include: /hire-me, /shop, /pdf-landing, a Buyer-A section on the home page, a "Stuck building?" section, etc.

### § 7 — Priority order
Top 5 things to ship first, in execution sequence. Each item: file path or new route, one-sentence description, why it matters.

### § 8 — Risks and tradeoffs
What this pivot puts at risk. Where the two buyers might tug against each other. What I'd be giving up by widening (e.g., does the AI-founder buyer get diluted? Does the corporate-hiring buyer get scared off by the vibe-coder audience?).

### § 9 — What you couldn't verify
Anything in this brief where you had to guess or extrapolate because the conversation memory or the site didn't carry the answer. Flag each one as a question for me to confirm.
